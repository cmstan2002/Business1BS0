# Edexcel GCSE Business 1BS0/01 — Paper 1 ("Investigating Small Business") Cross-Year Analysis

**Coverage:** Specimen Set 1 (SAMs Issue 2), Specimen Set 2, June 2019, Nov 2020, Nov 2021, June 2022, June 2023, June 2024, plus the 2021 New-Format Practice Paper.

**Spec context (Theme 1):** 1.1 Enterprise & entrepreneurship · 1.2 Spotting a business opportunity · 1.3 Putting a business idea into practice · 1.4 Making the business effective · 1.5 Understanding external influences.

---

## 1. Question Structure Consistency

Every Paper 1 sitting follows an identical three-section, 90-mark architecture. The headline change between 2017–2022 papers and 2023+ papers is a **+15-minute extension** (1h30 → 1h45) and the **migration of Section B/C case-study extracts into a separate Source Booklet**.

| Paper | Date | Time | Section A | Section B | Section C | MCQ items | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specimen Set 1 (SAMs) | — | 1h45 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | Source booklet (Meringue / Sally) |
| Specimen Set 2 | — | 1h30 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | In-paper extract (Zoella / POD Point) |
| June 2019 | 24 May 2019 | 1h30 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | In-paper extract (Last Course / Aphrodite) |
| Nov 2020 | 21 May 2020* | 1h30 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | In-paper (On Your Bike / Loco) |
| Nov 2021 | 2021 | 1h30 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | In-paper (Sports Tours / That Feeling) |
| June 2022 | 2022 | 1h30 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | In-paper (Little Movers / Digital Allies) |
| June 2023 | 18 May 2023 | **1h45** | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | **Source booklet** (Lili Heating / Ripple) |
| June 2024 | 14 May 2024 | 1h45 | 35 | 30 | 25 | 4 single + 2 multi | Source booklet (Adikoggz / PFC) |

\* The "Nov 2020" series sat the May 2020 paper after COVID disruption.

**Section structure (every paper):**

- **Section A (35)** = Q1 (8 marks) + Q2 (12 marks) + Q3 (15 marks). Three "stem" questions, each ending in a longer 6-mark "Discuss…" extended-response (Q3e); MCQs and 1–3 mark short answers fill the rest.
- **Section B (30)** = Q4 (8 marks) + Q5 (10 marks) + Q6 (12 marks). Q6 ends with a **9-mark "Justify…"** option choice.
- **Section C (25)** = Q7 (25 marks). Sub-parts (a)(1) → (b)(1) → (c)(2) → (d)(9 Justify) → (e)(12 Evaluate). The single exception is **June 2019**, where the 9-mark Section B ended Q6c, and the 12-mark Section C Evaluate was placed at Q7e (same overall structure but Q7d carries 9 marks of Justify only since 2020 onwards). 

**Mark sizes used (universal):** 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 12.

## 3. Guaranteed Question Types (every Paper 1)

Across all 8 papers analysed, the following items appear in **every** paper:

- **Q1(a) and Q1(b)**: two single-answer MCQs (1 mark each) — finance/economy/marketing definition territory.
- **Q1(c) or 1(d)**: two 3-mark "Explain one…" short-answer questions.
- **Q2(a) and Q2(b)**: two multiple-choice "select two" questions (2 marks each) — usually testing definitions/category sorting (e.g. variable costs, primary research, cash inflows).
- **Q2(c)**: a 2-mark **calculation** based on a Table 1 of business financials.
- **Q2(d)/(e)**: two more 3-mark "Explain" questions.
- **Q3(a)**: one further single MCQ (1 mark).
- **Q3(b)**: a 2-mark **calculation** based on a Figure 1 chart (always a bar chart or similar).
- **Q3(c)/(d)**: two 3-mark "Explain" questions.
- **Q3(e)**: a **6-mark "Discuss"** extended response — generic (no case-study).
- **Section B Q4(a)**: 2-mark "Outline one…" applied to the Section B business (italicised name = AO2 marker).
- **Section B Q4(b)**: 6-mark "Analyse the impact on [business] of…".
- **Section B Q5**: a 2-mark + 2-mark + 6-mark sandwich. The two 2-markers are **calculations** (so each paper has roughly 4 calculations total in Sections A+B). Q5(c) is another 6-mark "Analyse".
- **Section B Q6(a)**: 1-mark "State one…" applied (AO2).
- **Section B Q6(b)**: 2-mark "Outline one…" applied.
- **Section B Q6(c)**: **9-mark "Justify which one of these two options…"** — the first big extended response.
- **Section C Q7(a)**: 1-mark "State one…" applied.
- **Section C Q7(b)**: 1-mark "Identify…" from a chart/table (AO2 quantitative).
- **Section C Q7(c)**: 2-mark "Outline one…".
- **Section C Q7(d)**: **9-mark "Justify…" two-option choice** (since 2020).
- **Section C Q7(e)**: **12-mark "Evaluate…"** synoptic, using "the information provided as well as your knowledge of business".

Section A always contains **6 "Explain one…" questions** worth 3 marks each (= 18 marks) — examiners' reports flag this as the highest-yield bucket students lose marks on through poor technique.

## 4. Unique / One-Off Questions

These appeared once in the dataset and are useful as low-frequency revision items:

- **June 2019**: 12-mark Evaluate sat at Q7e in Section C **but** Section B Q6c was a 9-mark Justify on machinery vs employing more staff (the "expansion route" framing only appears once in this exact form).
- **June 2019 Q1(a)**: Definition of an exchange rate as an MCQ.
- **June 2019 Q3(b)**: Bar chart asking for **total profit** across three months (revenue minus cost interpretation).
- **June 2019 Q5(b)**: Calculate variable cost per unit after a **4% supplier price rise** (only paper to require a percentage uplift on a unit cost).
- **Nov 2020 Q5(a)**: Calculate **selling price per bike** working backwards from total revenue and unit sales — unique direction of calculation.
- **Nov 2020 Q5(b)**: Calculate total profit from forecast.
- **Nov 2021 Q2(c)**: First and only paper to require **completing a partial cash-flow forecast table** (two missing figures: a closing balance and a net cash flow). This was a one-off "fill in the table" calc.
- **Nov 2021 Q5(a)/(b)**: Two consecutive **exchange-rate £/€ price conversions** (€100 hotel and €130 hotel) — only paper with a back-to-back currency conversion pair.
- **Nov 2021 Q3(e)**: Discuss "impact of new employment laws" — only Section A 6-mark on legislation.
- **June 2022 Q5(a)/(b)**: Calculate **interest as a % of total amount borrowed** plus calculate **monthly loan repayment to 2 d.p.** — uniquely loan-mathematics-heavy.
- **June 2022 Q3(e)**: 6-mark Discuss on "conflicts between owners and employees and the effect on profit" (stakeholder conflict only appeared as a 6-marker once).
- **June 2023 Q5(b)**: Calculate **% of population in age group 45–49 that are female** — unique demographic-segmentation calculation drawn from a population table.
- **June 2023 Q7(d)**: Government policy choice — "reduce tax paid by small businesses vs provide free training" — only paper to frame the Section C Justify around a **government intervention** rather than a business strategy choice.
- **June 2024 Q5(a)**: Calculate the **average** number of trainers customised per month — only paper to require a simple arithmetic mean as a 2-mark calc.
- **June 2024 Q3(a)**: MCQ asks for a **financial aim** ("Survival" being the correct answer among Independence/Personal satisfaction/Challenge) — first time aims appear as a Section A MCQ in this exact polarised form.
- **Specimen Set 1 Q6(b)**: **Shading the profit region** on a break-even diagram — graphical-skill answer never repeated in real exams.
- **Specimen Set 2 Q5(b)**: Calculate **average closing balance** across three shops — average across multiple branches is unique.
- **Specimen Set 2 Q7(b)**: Calculate **% market share** from a doughnut chart — only doughnut/pie chart in the dataset.
- **June 2019 Q7**: Section C carries a **12-mark Evaluate of e-commerce on a marketing mix** — only year where this is the closing question (later years move it to Q7e while this paper labelled it Q7e too — so really unique only in topic of "e-commerce on the 4Ps", not position).

## 9. Command-Word Trends

Counts across 8 papers analysed (Section A + B + C combined). Each is a roughly stable per-paper number.

| Command word | Per-paper count | Total over 8 papers | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Select / Which one (MCQ) | 4 | 32 | 1 or 2 marks |
| State | 2 | 16 | Always AO2 — generic answer = 0 marks |
| Identify | 1 | 8 | Always from a chart/table (Q7b) |
| Outline | 3 | 24 | Always 2 marks |
| Explain | 6 | 48 | Always 3 marks; the technique-critical bucket |
| Calculate | 4 | 32 | Always 2 marks; covers ~10% of total marks |
| Discuss | 1 | 8 | Always 6 marks, Section A Q3e |

**Command-word stability is extremely high** — no paper deviates from this distribution by more than one item. The 2019 paper is the only one with a slightly different Justify/Evaluate placement.

| 2022 | Cash-flow line graph in/out flows May–Aug (Q3); table of break-even data (Q2); table of loan data (Q5); pie chart of UK social media accounts (Q7b) |

| 2024 | Bar chart units sold June–Aug (Q3); table of monthly trainers customised (Q5a/b); regional gym membership % table (Q7b) |

**Chart types ranked by frequency:**
1. Bar chart (every paper) — usually for revenue/costs/units/sales data.
2. Table of financial data (every paper) — for break-even, cash flow, or loan structure.
3. Pie or doughnut chart (3 papers: SAMs S2, 2022, occasionally implied) — market share / social-media share.
4. Line chart (2: 2019 interest, 2022 cash flow).
5. Cash-flow table with missing cells to complete (1: Nov 2021 — only paper to require completion of a partial table).
6. Customer-review/website screenshot used as qualitative stimulus for an Outline question (Nov 2020, SAMs Set 1).

Students are also routinely required to extract a **single value or comparison** from these charts — e.g. "which month had the lowest level…", "identify the second-largest social-media platform", "identify the type of energy use that declined the most", "identify the percentage of entrepreneurs in same age range as Justin".

| 20 | **Market segmentation** (1.2.3) | 5/8 | 6 | 0.8 |

**Headline takeaway:** the marketing mix, sources of finance, and technology together carry about a third of every paper. Break-even / cash flow / revenue-cost arithmetic carry the calculation marks (~10% guaranteed). The economy (1.5.4) is reliably present as a 6-mark Analyse or as the framing for the 12-mark Evaluate (e.g. June 2024 evaluated importance of consumer income; 2019 evaluated changing consumer income via Q5c).

## Appendix: Examiner-Report Recurring Advice (cross-year)

The Nov 2020, Nov 2021 and June 2024 ERs all converge on the same five points (these are reliable revision rules):

1. **Memorise Appendix 3 formulae.** Calculate/Identify questions = 10% of marks. The correct figure on the answer line scores both marks even without workings.
2. **Use BLT linking words** ("Because", "Leads to", "…to") on Explain questions to make two clear development strands. Without two strands, max = 2/3.
3. **State questions test AO2 only** — generic answers (e.g. "salaries", "age", "customers") score 0. The italicised business name in the question signals you must apply.
4. **In Justify questions, choose ONE option and develop it both ways.** Listing benefits-vs-benefits or drawbacks-vs-drawbacks across both options scores Level 0 for AO3b. The discarded option's drawbacks can support the chosen one.
5. **The 12-mark Evaluate conclusion must bring NEW evaluative content** (the "it depends…" rule, magnitude/timing/significance comparisons). A summary-only conclusion caps you at Level 2.

The most common dropped-mark pattern across every year is **failure to apply** — case-study context is the single biggest source of unscored AO2 marks in Sections B and C.
