# Paper 2 - Key Terms (Theme 2: Building a business)

Spaced repetition flashcards covering all five Theme 2 sub-topics. Question, then `?`, then answer, separated by `---`.

---

Name the two main methods of business growth.
?
Internal (organic) growth — expanding using the firm's own resources (new outlets, new products, e-commerce). External (inorganic) growth — joining with another business by merger or takeover.

---

What is the difference between a merger and a takeover?
?
Merger = two businesses agree to join together to form one new business (e.g. equal partners). Takeover (acquisition) = one business buys a controlling interest in another, often without full agreement.

---

Define organic growth and give two examples.
?
Growth using a business's own resources rather than buying another firm. Examples: opening new branches, launching new products, expanding online (e-commerce), entering new markets abroad.

---

What is a public limited company (plc)?
?
An incorporated business whose shares are sold to the general public on the stock market. It has limited liability, can raise large amounts of capital, but accounts must be public and ownership can change.

---

State two differences between a plc and an Ltd.
?
1) Plc shares are traded publicly on the stock exchange; Ltd shares are sold privately to known individuals. 2) Plcs require a minimum £50,000 share capital and must publish full accounts; Ltds face fewer disclosure rules.

---

What are the three elements of the design mix?
?
1) Function (does it work / do its job well), 2) Aesthetics (does it look/feel appealing), 3) Economic manufacture / Cost (can it be made profitably at the right price).

---

Name the four phases of the product life cycle.
?
1) Introduction (launch), 2) Growth (rising sales), 3) Maturity (peak sales, slowing growth), 4) Decline (falling sales).

---

Give two extension strategies a business uses to prolong the maturity phase of a product.
?
Repackaging/rebranding, lowering price, adding new features, finding new markets/uses, increasing promotion, releasing limited editions.

---

Define price skimming and price penetration.
?
Skimming = setting a high initial price for a new/unique product to maximise profit before competitors enter. Penetration = setting a low initial price to enter a market quickly, gain market share, then raise prices.

---

Define competitive pricing and price discrimination.
?
Competitive pricing = setting a price in line with rivals to stay competitive. Price discrimination = charging different customer groups different prices for the same product (e.g. peak vs off-peak train tickets).

---

Name the four main promotion methods.
?
1) Advertising (TV, social media, print), 2) Sponsorship, 3) Sales promotions (discounts, BOGOF, loyalty cards), 4) Branding/PR.

---

What are the three main production processes?
?
1) Job production — one-off bespoke items (wedding cake, bridge), 2) Batch production — groups of identical items in batches (bread loaves), 3) Flow production — continuous mass production on an assembly line (cars).

---

State one advantage and one disadvantage of flow production.
?
Advantage: low unit cost, high output, consistent quality through automation. Disadvantage: very high set-up costs, inflexible (hard to change product), demotivating repetitive work.

---

What does JIT (Just In Time) stock control mean?
?
Stock arrives from suppliers exactly when needed for production, holding minimum stock. Reduces storage costs and waste but relies on reliable suppliers — any delay halts production.

---

On a bar-gate stock graph, what is buffer stock?
?
The minimum level of stock a business holds at all times as a safety net to cover unexpected demand or delivery delays.

---

On a bar-gate stock graph, what is the reorder level?
?
The stock level at which a new order must be placed with suppliers so that delivery arrives before stock falls to (or below) the buffer level.

---

What is lead time on a bar-gate stock graph?
?
The time between placing an order with a supplier and the stock being delivered.

---

What is the difference between quality control and quality assurance?
?
Quality control = inspecting finished products at the end of production to remove defective ones. Quality assurance = building quality checks into every stage of production so defects are prevented before they happen.

---

State two methods a business uses to improve the customer experience.
?
1) Excellent customer service (trained, knowledgeable staff, fast response), 2) Easy and reliable post-sales support (returns, repairs, helpline, warranty).

---

State the formula for gross profit and gross profit margin.
?
Gross profit = sales revenue - cost of sales. Gross profit margin (%) = (gross profit / sales revenue) x 100.

---

State the formula for net profit and net profit margin.
?
Net profit = gross profit - other operating expenses and interest. Net profit margin (%) = (net profit / sales revenue) x 100.

---

State the formula for Average Rate of Return (ARR).
?
ARR (%) = (average annual profit / cost of investment) x 100, where average annual profit = total profit / number of years.

---

A £200,000 investment generates £500,000 total profit over 5 years. Calculate the ARR.
?
Average annual profit = £500,000 / 5 = £100,000. ARR = (£100,000 / £200,000) x 100 = 50%.

---

What is the difference between a hierarchical and a flat organisational structure?
?
Hierarchical = many layers of management, narrow span of control, clear chain of command (slow decisions). Flat = few layers, wide span of control, faster decisions and more autonomy for staff.

---

Define centralised and decentralised decision-making.
?
Centralised = decisions are made at the top by senior managers (consistency, control). Decentralised = decisions are delegated to lower levels / local managers (faster, more motivating, more responsive to local needs).

---

What is the difference between internal and external recruitment?
?
Internal = filling a vacancy from within the existing workforce (cheap, fast, motivating, but no fresh ideas). External = recruiting from outside the business (wider talent pool, fresh skills, but slower and more expensive).

---

What is the difference between a job description and a person specification?
?
Job description = the duties, responsibilities and tasks of the role. Person specification = the skills, qualifications, experience and personal qualities the ideal candidate should have.

---

Name three financial methods of motivation.
?
Remuneration (salary/wages), bonuses, commission, promotion (with pay rise), profit-sharing, fringe benefits (e.g. company car, healthcare).

---

Name three non-financial methods of motivation.
?
Job rotation (variety of tasks), job enrichment (more challenging/responsible tasks), autonomy (control over own work), praise/recognition, training, flexible working.

---

What is job rotation?
?
Moving employees between different tasks or jobs to reduce boredom from repetition, increase variety, and build a more flexible workforce.

---

What is job enrichment?
?
Giving employees more challenging, interesting and responsible tasks (added depth) so they feel valued and motivated rather than doing routine work.

---

Define globalisation and give one impact on UK businesses.
?
The increasing integration of national economies through trade, technology and movement of people. Impact: UK businesses access bigger markets abroad but face more competition from foreign rivals at home.

---

What is a tariff and what is its purpose?
?
A tax placed on imported goods. Purpose: to make imports more expensive, protect domestic producers, and raise government revenue.

---

State two ways a business can act ethically.
?
Pay fair wages (above minimum), use Fairtrade/sustainable suppliers, avoid child labour, reduce environmental impact, donate to charity, source locally.

---

What is a pressure group and give one example.
?
An organised group of people who campaign to influence business or government decisions on a particular issue. Example: Greenpeace (environment), PETA (animal welfare).

---

Why might a plc face conflict between short-term and long-term objectives?
?
Shareholders often want quick returns (short-term profit, dividends), but long-term success may require investment that reduces profits now (R&D, training, sustainability) — creating tension with management.

---

State one advantage and one disadvantage of a takeover.
?
Advantage: instant access to new markets, customers, technology, or economies of scale. Disadvantage: very expensive, risk of culture clash, may overpay for the target, possible job losses cause poor morale.

---

Why is good supplier relations important?
?
Reliable suppliers deliver on time, offer better prices/credit terms, maintain quality, and may give priority during shortages — all of which protects production and customer service.

---

What is e-commerce and why is it important to building a business?
?
Trading goods/services online. Allows businesses to reach a global market 24/7, lowers premises and staffing costs, collects customer data, and is essential for competing in modern retail.

---

Define remote/flexible working and give one benefit and one drawback.
?
Working away from the office (home/hybrid) or with non-standard hours. Benefit: lower premises costs, wider talent pool, higher motivation. Drawback: harder to communicate, manage and maintain team culture.
