# Paper 1 - Key Terms (Theme 1: Investigating small business)

Spaced repetition flashcards covering all five Theme 1 sub-topics. Question, then `?`, then answer, separated by `---`.

---

What is an entrepreneur?
?
An individual who organises the resources of a business, makes the key business decisions and takes the calculated risks of running a business in pursuit of reward (profit).

---

State three roles of an entrepreneur.
?
1) Organises the resources (land, labour, capital) of the business; 2) Makes business decisions (what to produce, how, who to employ); 3) Takes calculated risks in the hope of reward.

---

What is meant by the dynamic nature of business (1.1.1)?
?
Businesses change over time in response to changes in customer needs, technology and the wider economy — they must constantly adapt new ideas to existing products or develop new products to survive.

---

Define risk and reward in a business context.
?
Risk = the possibility of financial loss, business failure or lack of security if a decision goes wrong. Reward = the potential gain from taking the risk, usually profit, business growth or personal independence.

---

Why might a person choose to become an entrepreneur despite the risks?
?
Potential rewards include profit, independence (being your own boss), personal satisfaction, control of working hours, and the chance to turn a passion or hobby into income.

---

What does USP stand for and why is it important?
?
Unique Selling Point — the feature that distinguishes a business or product from competitors. It adds value, justifies a higher price, increases customer loyalty and helps build a brand.

---

What are customer needs and name four of them.
?
Customer needs are the wants/expectations a business must meet for customers to buy from it: 1) Price, 2) Quality, 3) Choice, 4) Convenience.

---

Define market research.
?
The process of gathering information about customers, competitors and the market in order to make informed business decisions and reduce risk.

---

What is the difference between primary and secondary market research?
?
Primary = first-hand data collected by the business itself (surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation). Secondary = data already collected by someone else (government statistics, market reports, internet, competitor websites).

---

Define market segmentation.
?
Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics (e.g. age, gender, income, location, lifestyle) so a business can target each group more effectively.

---

What is market mapping?
?
A diagram (usually a 2-axis chart) that plots competing products against two characteristics (e.g. price vs quality) to identify gaps in the market and competitor positioning.

---

What is the competitive environment?
?
The range of rival businesses operating in the same market. Understanding it helps a business identify its strengths/weaknesses and plan how to compete on price, quality, USP, customer service or location.

---

Define a business aim and give an example of a financial and a non-financial aim.
?
A business aim is a long-term goal. Financial example: survival, profit, growth, market share. Non-financial example: personal satisfaction, independence, social/ethical objectives, helping the community.

---

State the formula for total revenue and the formula for total costs.
?
Total revenue = price x quantity sold. Total costs (TC) = total fixed costs (TFC) + total variable costs (TVC).

---

What is the difference between fixed costs and variable costs?
?
Fixed costs do not change with output (e.g. rent, salaries, insurance). Variable costs change directly with the level of output (e.g. raw materials, packaging, hourly wages).

---

State the break-even formula.
?
Break-even point in units = fixed costs / (selling price per unit - variable cost per unit).

---

What is the margin of safety?
?
Margin of safety = actual (or budgeted) sales - break-even sales. It shows how much sales can fall before the business makes a loss.

---

Define cash flow and give the formula for net cash flow.
?
Cash flow = the movement of money into and out of a business over a period. Net cash flow = cash inflows - cash outflows in a given period.

---

What is the formula for closing balance on a cash-flow forecast?
?
Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow. (The closing balance of one period becomes the opening balance of the next.)

---

Why is cash flow critical to the survival of a small business?
?
Without enough cash, a business cannot pay suppliers, staff or bills, even if it is profitable on paper. Poor cash flow is the most common reason small businesses fail (insolvency).

---

Name five short-term and long-term sources of finance for a small business.
?
Short-term: overdraft, trade credit, retained profit. Long-term: bank loan, share capital (for Ltd), crowdfunding, personal savings, venture capital, leasing, grants.

---

What is a sole trader?
?
A business owned and run by one person, with unlimited liability. Easy and cheap to set up, owner keeps all profit, but bears all risk personally.

---

What is a partnership?
?
A business owned by 2-20 partners who share decisions, profits and unlimited liability. Set up with a Deed of Partnership.

---

Why might a business choose to be a partnership over a sole trader?
?
Partners share the workload, bring in extra skills/expertise, contribute more capital, and share the risk and decision-making — reducing pressure on a single owner.

---

What is a private limited company (Ltd)?
?
An incorporated business owned by shareholders (often family/friends) with shares not sold publicly. It has limited liability and is a separate legal entity from its owners.

---

Define limited liability.
?
Shareholders are only liable for the amount they invested in the business — their personal assets are protected if the business fails.

---

What is franchising?
?
A franchisee pays a franchisor for the right to use the franchisor's brand, products and business model in return for a fee and a share of profits (e.g. McDonald's, Subway).

---

Define business location and name three factors influencing it.
?
The geographical place where a business operates. Factors: proximity to customers, proximity to suppliers/raw materials, cost of premises, availability of labour, transport links, competition.

---

What are the four elements of the marketing mix (4 Ps)?
?
Product, Price, Place, Promotion — the combined factors a business uses to influence customers to buy.

---

Why must the four elements of the marketing mix work together?
?
Customers form an overall impression. A premium product needs a premium price, prestige promotion and an upmarket place — inconsistency confuses customers and weakens the brand.

---

What is a business plan and what is its main purpose?
?
A written document setting out aims, target market, marketing mix, finance and operations of a proposed business. It reduces risk, secures finance from lenders, and provides direction.

---

Name four sections of a typical business plan.
?
1) Business idea and aims/objectives, 2) Target market and market research, 3) Marketing mix, 4) Financial forecasts (cash flow, break-even), plus location and resources.

---

Define stakeholder and give four examples.
?
Any individual or group with an interest in the activities of a business. Examples: owners/shareholders, employees, customers, suppliers, the local community, government, pressure groups.

---

How can the objectives of owners and employees conflict?
?
Owners want to maximise profit (e.g. lower wages, longer hours), while employees want higher pay, better conditions and job security. Conflict can reduce motivation and productivity.

---

What is e-commerce and why is it important to small businesses?
?
Buying and selling goods/services online. It allows small businesses to reach a global market, trade 24/7, reduce premises costs, and compete with larger rivals.

---

Name three ways technology has changed how small businesses operate.
?
1) E-commerce / online sales reach more customers; 2) Social media marketing is cheap and targeted; 3) Digital communication and payment systems (e.g. PayPal) speed up transactions.

---

State two pieces of consumer protection legislation a small business must follow.
?
Sale of Goods Act / Consumer Rights Act 2015 (goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described); Trade Descriptions Act (claims about products must be truthful).

---

State two areas of employment law that affect small businesses.
?
Pay (national minimum wage), discrimination (Equality Act 2010 — no discrimination by gender, race, age, disability), plus health & safety and contracts of employment.

---

How does a rise in interest rates affect a small business?
?
Loan repayments become more expensive, so borrowing falls and existing debt costs more. Customers also have less disposable income (mortgage costs rise), so demand for non-essentials falls.

---

How does a fall in the exchange rate (weaker pound) affect a UK small business?
?
Exports become cheaper for foreign buyers (good for exporters), but imports become more expensive (bad for businesses that buy raw materials from abroad).

---

Define unemployment and explain one effect on small businesses.
?
Unemployment = the number of people willing and able to work but unable to find a job. High unemployment means lower consumer income and demand falls, but it also makes it easier and cheaper to recruit staff.
