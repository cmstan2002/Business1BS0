# Case Studies & Exam Format

Cards covering the recurring case-study businesses on Paper 2 (and notable Paper 1 cases) plus exam format facts. Question, then `?`, then answer, separated by `---`.

---

How long is each Edexcel GCSE Business 1BS0 paper?
?
1 hour 45 minutes (extended from 1h30 for Paper 1 in 2023; Paper 2 went to 1h45 from the same point).

---

How many marks is each paper worth?
?
90 marks per paper — 180 marks total.

---

What % of the qualification is each paper?
?
50% each — Paper 1 is 50%, Paper 2 is 50%.

---

How is each paper split into sections?
?
Section A = 35 marks (no case study), Section B = 30 marks (case study), Section C = 25 marks (case study).

---

What command word is used for the 12-mark question, and where does it appear?
?
Evaluate — always Q7(e), the final question of Paper 1 and Paper 2.

---

What command word is used for the 9-mark question?
?
Justify — appears twice per paper (Q6c in Section B and Q7d in Section C), choosing between Option 1 and Option 2.

---

What command word is used for the 6-mark non-case-study question?
?
Discuss — always Q3(e) in Section A, no case-study context.

---

What command word is used for the 6-mark Section B case-study question?
?
Analyse — applied to the named Section B business at Q4(b) and Q5(c).

---

How many calculations appear on Paper 1?
?
Four calculations (each 2 marks) plus one Identify-from-graph (1 mark) — 9 marks total of quantitative skills, ~10% of the paper.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2024?
?
Cineworld plc — the 12-mark Evaluate asked whether charging lower prices would allow Cineworld to return to profit.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2023?
?
Facebook — the 12-mark Evaluate was on the impact of allowing employees to use remote working from home.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2022?
?
JD Sports plc — the 12-mark Evaluate was on whether the takeover of Footasylum would allow JD Sports to increase profit.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2021?
?
ASOS plc — the 12-mark Evaluate asked whether high quality logistics would give ASOS a competitive advantage.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2020?
?
Iceland — the 12-mark Evaluate asked whether Iceland would benefit from its decision to ban all plastic packaging on own-brand products.

---

Which business was tested in Paper 2 Section C in 2019?
?
Sainsbury's plc — the 12-mark Evaluate was on the impact of replacing Fairtrade with its own "Fairly Traded" scheme.

---

Which businesses were tested in Paper 2 Section B from 2019-2024?
?
Mind Candy (2019), KFC (2020), Tesla (2021), Greggs (2022), Ocado (2023), Popeyes (2024) — household-name mid-cap retailers/manufacturers.

---

Which case studies appeared in Paper 1 Section B and C in 2024?
?
Section B: Adikoggz (sole-trader customised trainers). Section C: PFC (independent gym facing chain competition).

---

Which case studies appeared in Paper 1 Section B and C in 2023?
?
Section B: Lili Heating Ltd (female plumber start-up). Section C: Ripple (consumer-owned green energy / wind farm).

---

What was Iceland's strategic dilemma in the 2020 Paper 2 Section C?
?
Whether to ban all plastic packaging on its own-brand products by 2023 — balancing environmental/ethical pressure-group demands and brand image vs higher costs and supplier risk.

---

What was JD Sports' strategic dilemma in the 2022 Paper 2 Section C?
?
Whether the takeover of Footasylum would increase its profit — assessing growth via acquisition, regulator (CMA) intervention, and integration risks.

---

Why does the examiner reward students who name the actual business in their answer?
?
Application marks (AO2) require contextual reference. Writing "Cineworld should…" instead of "the business should…" earns AO2 marks; generic answers cap at low Levels even with strong analysis.

---

What two phrases must appear in a strong 12-mark Evaluate conclusion?
?
"It depends on…" plus a contextual factor from the case (e.g. size of competitor, time horizon, level of demand). The conclusion must add NEW evaluative content — not just summarise.
