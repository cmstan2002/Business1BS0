# Revision Checklist

Every sub-topic in the Edexcel GCSE Business spec (1BS0). Tick a box only when you can: (a) define the term, (b) give a worked example, and (c) apply it to a Section B/C case study.

> [!tip] How to use
> - Work through one sub-section per session.
> - Cross-reference the topic note in `[[Topics/]]` and any flashcards in `[[Flashcards/]]`.

## Theme 1 — Investigating small business (Paper 1)

### 1.1 Enterprise and entrepreneurship

#### 1.1.1 The dynamic nature of business
- [ ] Why new business ideas come about: changes in technology
- [ ] Why new business ideas come about: changes in what consumers want
- [ ] Why new business ideas come about: products/services becoming obsolete
- [ ] How new business ideas come about: original ideas vs adapting existing products/services

#### 1.1.2 Risk and reward
- [ ] Risks: business failure, financial loss, lack of security
- [ ] Rewards: business success, profit, independence, self-esteem
- [ ] Trade-off between risk and reward

#### 1.1.3 The role of business enterprise and entrepreneurship
- [ ] Producing goods/services
- [ ] Meeting customer needs
- [ ] Adding value: convenience, branding, quality, design, USP
- [ ] Role of the entrepreneur: organising resources, making decisions, taking risks

### 1.2 Spotting a business opportunity

#### 1.2.1 Customer needs
- [ ] What customers want: price, quality, choice, convenience
- [ ] Importance of identifying and meeting customer needs

#### 1.2.2 Market research
- [ ] Purpose: identify needs, identify gaps, reduce risk, inform decisions
- [ ] Primary methods: survey, questionnaire, focus group, observation
- [ ] Secondary methods: internet, government reports, market reports
- [ ] Quantitative vs qualitative data
- [ ] Use of social media for market research
- [ ] Reliability of data

#### 1.2.3 Market segmentation
- [ ] By location, demographics (age, gender, income), lifestyle, income, age
- [ ] How segmentation helps a business target customers

#### 1.2.4 The competitive environment
- [ ] Identifying competitors (price, quality, location, product range, customer service)
- [ ] Strengths and weaknesses of competitors
- [ ] Impact of competition on business decision-making

### 1.3 Putting a business idea into practice

#### 1.3.1 Business aims and objectives
- [ ] Financial aims: survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security
- [ ] Non-financial aims: social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence, control
- [ ] Why aims and objectives differ between businesses

#### 1.3.2 Business revenues, costs and profits
- [ ] Revenue = price × quantity
- [ ] Fixed costs vs variable costs; total costs
- [ ] Profit = total revenue − total costs
- [ ] Interest as a cost of borrowing

#### 1.3.3 Break-even
- [ ] Break-even level of output formula: FC / (selling price − VC per unit)
- [ ] Margin of safety
- [ ] Drawing/interpreting break-even charts (Paper 1 only)
- [ ] Impact of changes in revenue/costs on break-even

#### 1.3.4 Cash and cash flow
- [ ] Importance of cash to a business (paying suppliers, wages, rent)
- [ ] Cash inflows vs outflows
- [ ] Cash flow forecast: opening balance, net cash flow, closing balance
- [ ] Filling in a partial cash-flow forecast

#### 1.3.5 Sources of business finance
- [ ] Short-term: overdraft, trade credit
- [ ] Long-term: personal savings, venture capital, share capital, loans, retained profit, crowdfunding
- [ ] Suitability of each source for different business situations

### 1.4 Making the business effective

#### 1.4.1 The options for start-up and small businesses
- [ ] Sole trader: features, advantages, disadvantages
- [ ] Partnership: features, advantages, disadvantages
- [ ] Private limited company (Ltd): features, limited liability, share capital
- [ ] Franchising: franchisor and franchisee benefits and drawbacks

#### 1.4.2 Business location
- [ ] Factors influencing location: proximity to market, labour, materials, competitors
- [ ] Impact of the internet on location decisions (e-commerce, working from home)

#### 1.4.3 The marketing mix
- [ ] Product (design, USP, branding)
- [ ] Price (pricing strategies and impact on demand)
- [ ] Promotion (advertising, sponsorship, sales promotion, social media)
- [ ] Place (distribution channels, e-commerce, physical retail)
- [ ] How the elements interact and influence business decisions

#### 1.4.4 Business plans
- [ ] Purpose: clarify ideas, identify risk, secure finance
- [ ] Typical contents: aims/objectives, target market, marketing mix, finance, location

### 1.5 Understanding external influences on business

#### 1.5.1 Business stakeholders
- [ ] Owners, employees, customers, suppliers, local community, government, pressure groups
- [ ] Stakeholder objectives and conflicts

#### 1.5.2 Technology and business
- [ ] E-commerce, social media, digital communication, payment systems
- [ ] Impact on costs, sales, marketing mix, customer experience

#### 1.5.3 Legislation and business
- [ ] Principles of consumer law (Sale of Goods, Trade Descriptions)
- [ ] Principles of employment law (recruitment, pay, discrimination, working hours, health & safety)
- [ ] Impact of legislation on business costs, decisions, and reputation

#### 1.5.4 The economy and business
- [ ] Unemployment levels
- [ ] Changing levels of consumer income
- [ ] Inflation
- [ ] Changes in interest rates
- [ ] Government taxation
- [ ] Exchange rates (SPICED / WIDEC) — converting currencies

#### 1.5.5 External influences (broader)
- [ ] How external influences interact (e.g. technology + legislation)
- [ ] How a small business responds to external change

## Theme 2 — Building a business (Paper 2)

### 2.1 Growing the business

#### 2.1.1 Business growth
- [ ] Internal (organic) growth: new products, new markets, e-commerce
- [ ] External (inorganic) growth: mergers, takeovers
- [ ] Public limited company (plc): features, advantages, disadvantages
- [ ] Sources of finance for growth: retained profit, share capital, loan capital, selling assets

#### 2.1.2 Changes in business aims and objectives
- [ ] Why aims/objectives change as a business grows (market conditions, performance, legislation, internal reasons)
- [ ] How aims/objectives change (focus on profit, expansion, new markets)

#### 2.1.3 Business and globalisation
- [ ] Imports and exports
- [ ] Multinationals
- [ ] Tariffs and trade blocs
- [ ] How globalisation affects businesses (competition, supply chains, marketing, location)

#### 2.1.4 Ethics, the environment and business
- [ ] Ethical considerations: Fairtrade, supply chain, working conditions
- [ ] Environmental considerations: pollution, packaging, sustainability, climate change
- [ ] Trade-offs between ethics, environment and profit
- [ ] Pressure groups and their impact

### 2.2 Making marketing decisions

#### 2.2.1 Product
- [ ] Design mix: function, aesthetics, economic manufacture
- [ ] Product life cycle stages: introduction, growth, maturity, decline
- [ ] Extension strategies: re-launch, new markets, new packaging, advertising
- [ ] Importance of differentiation

#### 2.2.2 Price
- [ ] Pricing strategies: cost-plus, price skimming, penetration, competitive, promotional, psychological
- [ ] Factors influencing pricing: tech, competition, market segments, costs, product life cycle

#### 2.2.3 Promotion
- [ ] Methods: advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding, social media, viral advertising
- [ ] Use of technology in promotion: targeted ads, e-newsletters, viral campaigns

#### 2.2.4 Place
- [ ] Methods of distribution: retailers, e-tailers (e-commerce), direct
- [ ] Impact of e-commerce on the marketing mix

#### 2.2.5 Using the marketing mix to make business decisions
- [ ] How the four elements work together
- [ ] Influence of the marketing mix on business performance

### 2.3 Making operational decisions

#### 2.3.1 Business operations
- [ ] Production processes: job, batch, flow
- [ ] Impact of technology on production (automation, balancing efficiency vs cost)

#### 2.3.2 Working with suppliers
- [ ] Managing stock: bar-gate stock graphs (re-order level, buffer stock, lead time, max stock)
- [ ] Reading bar-gate graphs: order size, days out of stock, total delivered
- [ ] Just-in-time (JIT) stock control vs holding buffer stock
- [ ] Role of procurement and logistics
- [ ] Impact of supplier relationships on cost, quality, delivery

#### 2.3.3 Managing quality
- [ ] Quality control vs quality assurance
- [ ] Importance of quality to consumers and businesses
- [ ] Cost of quality and impact on reputation

#### 2.3.4 The sales process
- [ ] Stages: customer engagement, speed/efficiency of service, customer feedback, post-sales service
- [ ] Importance of good customer service
- [ ] Impact of poor customer service on competitiveness

### 2.4 Making financial decisions

#### 2.4.1 Business calculations
- [ ] Gross profit = sales revenue − cost of sales
- [ ] Net profit = gross profit − other operating expenses and interest
- [ ] Gross profit margin = (gross profit / sales revenue) × 100
- [ ] Net profit margin = (net profit / sales revenue) × 100
- [ ] Average rate of return = (average annual profit / cost of investment) × 100
- [ ] Calculation of average annual profit when given total profit and project life

#### 2.4.2 Understanding business performance
- [ ] Use of quantitative data: sales, market share, profit margins, ARR
- [ ] Use of qualitative data: customer feedback, brand image
- [ ] Limitations of financial data (ignores non-financial factors)

### 2.5 Making human resource decisions

#### 2.5.1 Organisational structures
- [ ] Hierarchical vs flat structures (chain of command, span of control)
- [ ] Centralised vs decentralised decision-making
- [ ] Importance of effective communication; barriers to communication
- [ ] Impact of different structures on motivation and efficiency

#### 2.5.2 Effective recruitment
- [ ] Different job roles: directors, managers, supervisors, operatives, support staff
- [ ] Recruitment documents: person specification, job description, application form, CV
- [ ] Internal vs external recruitment: advantages and disadvantages

#### 2.5.3 Effective training and development
- [ ] Methods: formal/informal, ongoing training, target setting, performance reviews
- [ ] Why businesses train and develop staff: retention, motivation, performance

#### 2.5.4 Motivation
- [ ] Importance of motivation in the workplace
- [ ] Financial methods: remuneration (wages/salaries), commission, bonuses, promotion, fringe benefits
- [ ] Non-financial methods: job rotation, job enrichment, autonomy, recognition
- [ ] Permanent vs temporary contracts; full-time vs flexible/part-time
