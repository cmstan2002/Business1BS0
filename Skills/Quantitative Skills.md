# Quantitative Skills

> [!info] Spec source
> Every formula below is from **Appendix 3 (page 32) of the Edexcel GCSE Business 1BS0 Specification (Issue 2)**. The spec confirms: **"Formulae will not be provided in the examinations for Paper 1 or Paper 2."** Memorise them. Plus % change and average — used regularly in papers but not in the formula list.

## The complete formula bank

### 1. Total costs
**TC = TFC + TVC**
*Worked example:* fixed costs £2,000/month + variable cost £4 × 500 units → TC = £2,000 + £2,000 = **£4,000**

### 2. Revenue
**Revenue = price × quantity**
*Worked example:* 800 trainers at £45 each → Revenue = 800 × £45 = **£36,000**

### 3. Break-even point
**BEP (units) = fixed cost ÷ (selling price − variable cost per unit)**
**BEP (£) = BEP (units) × selling price**
*Worked example:* fixed costs £6,000; price £20; variable cost £8 → contribution per unit = £12 → BEP = 6,000 / 12 = **500 units**

### 4. Margin of safety
**Margin of safety = actual (or budgeted) sales − break-even sales** (in units)
*Worked example:* selling 750 units; BEP = 500 → MoS = 750 − 500 = **250 units**

### 5. Interest on loans
**Interest (%) = ((total repayment − borrowed amount) / borrowed amount) × 100**
*Worked example (SAMs Q5b):* £4,500 borrowed; 36 monthly payments of £136.50 = £4,914 total → interest = (4,914 − 4,500)/4,500 × 100 = **9.2%**

### 6. Net cash flow
**Net cash flow = cash inflows − cash outflows in a given period**
*Worked example:* inflows £8,000; outflows £6,500 → net cash flow = **+£1,500**

### 7. Opening and closing balances
- **Opening balance = closing balance of the previous period**
- **Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow**

*Worked example:* April closing balance £2,000 → May opening £2,000; May net cash flow −£500 → May closing = £2,000 + (−500) = **£1,500**

### 8. Gross profit
**Gross profit = sales revenue − cost of sales**
*Worked example (Nov 2020 Q2c):* sales £625,000; cost of sales £145,000 → GP = **£480,000**

### 9. Gross profit margin
**GP margin (%) = (gross profit / sales revenue) × 100**
*Worked example (June 2019 Q5b):* GP £22,190,385; sales £30,560,692 → margin = (22,190,385/30,560,692) × 100 = **72.61%** (to 2dp)

### 10. Net profit
**Net profit = gross profit − other operating expenses and interest**
*Worked example (Nov 2021 Q2c):* GP £575,000; expenses £200,000 → NP = **£375,000**

### 11. Net profit margin
**NP margin (%) = (net profit / sales revenue) × 100**
*Worked example (June 2023 Q2c):* NP £400,000; sales £900,000 → margin = (400,000/900,000) × 100 = **44.44%** (to 2dp)

### 12. Average rate of return (ARR)
**ARR (%) = (average annual profit / cost of investment) × 100**
where average annual profit = total profit ÷ number of years
*Worked example (June 2024 Q2c):* total profit £500,000 over 5 years → average annual profit = £100,000; cost £200,000 → ARR = (100,000/200,000) × 100 = **50%**

## Bonus formulae used regularly but NOT on the spec list

### % change
**% change = ((new value − old value) / old value) × 100**
*Worked example (June 2019 Q3b):* 2015 sales £750,000 → 2016 sales £1,500,000 → % change = (750,000/750,000) × 100 = **100%**

### Average (arithmetic mean)
**Average = sum of values / number of values**
*Worked example (June 2024 Q5a):* prices £6.50, £6.25, £1.00, £3.50, £2.75 → average = 20.00 / 5 = **£4.00**

### % of a total / proportion
**% of total = (part / total) × 100**
*Worked example (June 2024 Q5b):* chicken wings cost £6.25 of £20.00 order → (6.25/20.00) × 100 = **31.25%**

### Currency conversion
**Cost in £ = cost in € / exchange rate (€ per £)**
*Worked example (Nov 2021 Q5):* €100 hotel; rate €1.15/£1 → £ cost = 100 / 1.15 = **£86.96**

## Calculator rules (Appendix 4)
- Calculator is **allowed**.
- It must be free of stored formulae and not have algebraic-symbol manipulation.
- No internet/data connection.
- You are responsible for batteries.

## Examiner-flagged habits
> [!warning] Three things that lose easy marks
> 1. **No workings shown** — even with a correct final figure, blank workings can cap the mark in some calc styles. Always write the formula and the substitution.
> 2. **Wrong units** — answer "£" when % is required, or vice versa. **Always write £/%/units** next to your final figure.
> 3. **Rounding too early** — keep full precision until the last step; round only at the very end (and to the d.p. asked).
