# Extended Response Structures

> [!info] Why this matters
> The 6-mark Discuss/Analyse, the two 9-mark Justify, and the 12-mark Evaluate together carry **~45 of 90 marks per paper** — half the exam. Drilling these structures is the single highest-leverage thing Nicole can do.

## 1. PEEL — the engine of every extended response

- **P**oint: state the idea you're making
- **E**vidence: quote/reference the case-study extract OR a worked business fact
- **E**xplain: chain of cause-and-effect ("because… leads to… which means…")
- **L**ink: tie the chain back to the **specific business** and the question

A 6-mark Discuss = roughly two short PEELs OR one developed PEEL. A 6-mark Analyse needs application to the named business in *every* PEEL.

## 2. The 6-mark Discuss / Analyse structure

**Discuss (Section A Q3e)** — no case study. Time: ~6 minutes.

```
[1-sentence intro stating the issue]
P – One impact is that…
E – For example…
E – This means / leads to…
L – Therefore for a small business this is significant because…

[Optional balancing point — 1–2 sentences on the other side]
[Mini-conclusion — 1 sentence]
```

**Analyse (Section B Q4b / Q5c)** — case-study based. Same structure, but EVERY paragraph must reference the named business and the extract.

## 3. The 9-mark Justify structure

> [!warning] Justify is ONE-SIDED
> Pick **one** option in your first sentence and defend it. Use the rejected option's weaknesses to *strengthen* your choice — do NOT write a balanced essay. Examiners cap balanced answers at Level 1.

```
INTRO (1 sentence)
"I would recommend [chosen option] for [named business] because…"

PEEL paragraph for the CHOSEN option (4–5 sentences)
P – Main benefit of chosen option
E – Specific evidence from the case study
E – Cause-and-effect chain (because… leads to… therefore…)
L – Tied to the business's stated aim/situation

REJECTION of the OTHER option (1–2 sentences)
"The alternative, [rejected option], is less suitable because [single weakness applied to this business]."

CONCLUSION (1 sentence)
"Overall, [chosen option] best supports [business]'s aim of [X], particularly given [contextual factor]."
```

**Mark-scheme weighting** (per examiner reports): 3 marks AO1 knowledge + 3 marks AO2 application + 3 marks AO3b judgement. **Skipping any one tier caps the mark.**

## 4. The 12-mark Evaluate structure

> [!tip] The "It depends on…" rule
> The conclusion must bring **NEW evaluative content** — not a summary. Use "It depends on…" + 1–2 contextual factors (size of business, time horizon, level of competition, magnitude of the change).

```
INTRO (1 sentence)
"Whether [issue/decision] benefits [business] depends on a number of factors."

PEEL paragraph 1 — argument FOR (4–5 sentences)
P – One reason it would benefit them
E – Specific evidence from the source
E – Cause-and-effect chain
L – Significance for the business

PEEL paragraph 2 — argument AGAINST / RISK (4–5 sentences)
P – One reason it might not benefit / risk involved
E – Specific evidence
E – Chain
L – Significance

CONCLUSION (3–4 sentences) — THE MARKS-MAKING SECTION
"Overall, [business] is/is not likely to benefit, but this depends on:
- [Factor 1, e.g. how long-term we measure success]
- [Factor 2, e.g. how rivals respond]
On balance, given [the business's specific context — size, market position, recent strategy], the [decision] is likely to [judgement] because [final reason]."
```

## 5. Worked Example — 9-mark Justify

> [!example] Question (June 2024 Paper 1 Q7d-style)
> *Adikoggz is choosing between two sources of finance for expansion: an overdraft from the bank, or trade credit from suppliers. Justify which one of these two options Adikoggz should choose. (9 marks)*

**Model answer:**

I would recommend Adikoggz takes **trade credit from suppliers** because the business is small with limited cash reserves and trade credit is the cheaper, lower-risk option at this stage of growth.

Trade credit means Adikoggz can receive raw materials (the trainers and customising materials needed) and pay 30–60 days later, allowing them to sell the customised trainers and use the cash from those sales to pay the supplier. This protects Adikoggz's cash flow because the business does not need to borrow at interest, and the founder's personal savings are not put at risk. Because Adikoggz started as a hobby and has only recently scaled, every pound of cash matters — trade credit effectively provides a free short-term loan that finances the working capital needed for expansion.

The alternative, an overdraft, would charge a high interest rate on every day the account is overdrawn, eating into Adikoggz's already-thin profit margins, and the bank could withdraw the facility at any time, which would be catastrophic for a small business mid-expansion.

Overall, trade credit better supports Adikoggz's aim of growing without taking on financial risk, particularly because the business has built supplier relationships during its hobby phase that make negotiating credit terms realistic.

**Why this scores L3:**
- Clear choice in opening sentence (AO3b judgement)
- Specific business context throughout (AO2 application)
- Two-stage chain of reasoning per paragraph (AO1 knowledge + AO3a analysis)
- One-sentence rejection of the alternative (NOT a balanced essay)
- Contextual conclusion
