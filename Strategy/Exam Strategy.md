# Exam Strategy

How to spend the 105 minutes of each paper, how to structure each extended-response question, and the recurring mistakes that cost most marks. Every recommendation here is grounded in the **examiner reports 2019-2024** (see `Archive/organized/<year>/...ExaminerReport.pdf`).

---

## 1. Time management

Each paper: **90 marks in 105 minutes**. Apply the **1 mark = 1 minute** rule, then bank the surplus.

| Block | Marks | Recommended time |
|---|---|---|
| Section A (Q1-Q3) | 35 | ~35 min |
| Section B (Q4-Q6) | 30 | ~30 min |
| Section C (Q7) | 25 | ~25 min |
| Reading the source booklet (Section B/C) | — | 5 min up front |
| Checking calculations + extended responses | — | 10 min at the end |
| **Total** | **90** | **105 min** |

> [!tip] Big-question minimums
> - **9-mark Justify:** 9 minutes minimum, 11 minutes max
> - **12-mark Evaluate:** 12 minutes minimum, 15 minutes max
> - **6-mark Analyse/Discuss:** 6-7 minutes
>
> Together, the two 9-markers and the 12-marker eat ~30 minutes of your paper — about a third of your time for a third of the marks. If you fall behind, **never** sacrifice these.

> [!warning] If you run out of time
> Always write a **conclusion** for the 12-marker. Examiners cap you at Level 2 if there is no "It depends on…" judgement, even if the body is excellent.

## 2. The 6-mark Discuss / Analyse — PEEL

PEEL = **P**oint, **E**vidence, **E**xplain, **L**ink. One PEEL paragraph for a 6-marker, two PEEL points for stronger answers.

| Step | What to write | Marks it earns |
|---|---|---|
| **P**oint | State the impact / advantage / disadvantage | AO1 (knowledge) |
| **E**vidence | Lift a number, name, or fact from the case study (Section B/C only) | AO2 (application) |
| **E**xplain | "This means that… because… leads to…" — chain of reasoning | AO3 (analysis) |
| **L**ink | Bring it back to the question; mention the business by name again | AO3 (analysis) |

> [!example] Worked PEEL — June 2024 Paper 1 Q5(c) "Analyse the advantage to Adikoggz from using social media to promote its products"
> **P** — Using social media will increase brand awareness for Adikoggz.
> **E** — Adikoggz is a small sole trader started as a hobby with limited marketing budget; social media is free or low-cost.
> **E** — This means Adikoggz can reach a much larger audience than via word-of-mouth, because platforms like Instagram allow customisation videos to go viral, leading to higher demand for its bespoke trainers.
> **L** — As a result, Adikoggz can grow sales without spending money on traditional advertising, which is critical for a sole trader still building its customer base.

For Section A 6-mark Discuss (no case study), drop the Evidence step — replace it with a generalised example ("for example, a sole-trader bakery…").

## 3. The 9-mark Justify — pick a side, defend it

> [!warning] Justify ≠ balanced essay
> Examiners' reports flag this every single year. **Justify** means *choose one option*, defend it with a fully developed PEEL, and **dismiss** the other in one sentence. Listing pros for both = Level 1 only.

### Structure (90 seconds to plan, 8 minutes to write)

1. **Intro (1 sentence):** "I would recommend [Business] choose Option [X] because…"
2. **Chosen-option PEEL paragraph #1:** Point + Case-study evidence + Develop the chain of reasoning
3. **Chosen-option PEEL paragraph #2:** A second reason, ideally drawing on a different element of the case (a number, a stakeholder, a competitor)
4. **Reject the other option in 1 line:** "Option [Y] is weaker because [single drawback drawn from the case]."
5. **Conclusion (1 sentence):** "Therefore [Business] should choose Option [X] in order to [contextual goal]."

> [!example] Worked example — June 2024 Paper 1 Q6(c)
> *"Justify which one of these two options would be the best way for **Adikoggz** to fund expansion: (1) An overdraft from the bank; (2) Trade credit from suppliers."*
>
> **Intro:** Adikoggz should choose **trade credit from suppliers** to fund expansion.
>
> **PEEL 1 — Point + Evidence:** Trade credit allows Adikoggz to receive raw materials (the trainers and customisation supplies) now and pay later, typically 30 days. Adikoggz is a sole trader still building cash reserves, so this delays cash outflows.
> **Explain:** This means Adikoggz can sell the customised trainers, collect the cash from customers, and then pay the supplier — improving cash flow without paying interest. Because Adikoggz operates on a hobby-turned-business basis, every pound of profit kept is critical for reinvestment.
>
> **PEEL 2 — Point + Evidence:** Trade credit also avoids the **interest costs** of an overdraft. Bank overdrafts charge high daily interest, especially for small businesses with no trading history.
> **Explain:** As Adikoggz only just started selling trainers, it cannot guarantee consistent monthly revenue. An overdraft would risk piling up interest charges in slow months, eating into the small profit margin on each pair of customised trainers.
>
> **Reject Option 1:** An overdraft is also short-term and the bank can demand repayment at any time, which would be catastrophic for a young business like Adikoggz.
>
> **Conclusion:** Therefore Adikoggz should choose trade credit from suppliers to fund expansion, because it preserves cash, avoids interest, and matches the small-business cash-flow cycle.

### What earns each mark band (per the mark scheme template)

| Level | Marks | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1-3 | Knowledge only — generic answer, no case-study application |
| L2 | 4-6 | Application + some chains of reasoning, but justification one-sided or shallow |
| L3 | 7-9 | Fully developed PEEL using case data, **explicit choice**, balanced rejection of other option, contextual conclusion |

## 4. The 12-mark Evaluate — PEEL × 2 + "It depends on…"

The Q7(e) Evaluate is the **single highest-scoring question** on each paper if you nail the structure.

### Structure (3 minutes to plan, 12 minutes to write)

1. **Intro (1 sentence):** Restate the question and signal you will weigh up two factors.
2. **PEEL paragraph #1 — argument FOR (or factor 1):** point, case evidence, chain of reasoning, link.
3. **PEEL paragraph #2 — argument AGAINST (or factor 2):** point, case evidence, chain of reasoning, link.
4. **"It depends on…" conclusion (3-4 sentences):**
   - State your overall judgement
   - Give 1-2 contextual factors that the answer **depends on** (e.g. how loyal existing customers are; how much cash reserves are; how price-sensitive the market is)
   - Reference magnitude / timing / significance ("In the short term… but in the long term…")

> [!example] Worked example — June 2024 Paper 2 Q7(e)
> *"Evaluate the importance of charging lower prices to allow **Cineworld** to return to profit."*
>
> **Intro:** Charging lower prices could help Cineworld return to profit by attracting more customers, but it carries significant risks given Cineworld's debt position and the loyalty of cinema-goers.
>
> **PEEL #1 — FOR lower prices:**
> **P** — Lower ticket prices increase price-elastic demand at multiplexes.
> **E** — Cineworld is competing against streaming services like Netflix that cost ~£10/month; cinema tickets at ~£12 each look poor value.
> **E** — Lowering prices would attract budget-conscious families and students back into cinemas because the perceived value gap closes; even small increases in attendance multiply revenue across food/drink concessions, which carry much higher margins than tickets.
> **L** — This could push Cineworld back into operating profit if attendance recovers to pre-pandemic levels.
>
> **PEEL #2 — AGAINST lower prices:**
> **P** — However, lower prices reduce revenue per ticket and may not cover Cineworld's huge fixed costs.
> **E** — Cineworld carries ~$8.9bn of debt and pays high lease costs on cinema premises in major cities.
> **E** — If demand does not increase enough to offset the price cut, total revenue falls; this means Cineworld may not generate enough cash to service its debt and could push it back toward insolvency. Premium IMAX customers may also perceive the cinema as "downmarket" and switch to rival chains.
> **L** — Therefore lower prices alone are unlikely to fix Cineworld's structural debt problem.
>
> **Conclusion / "It depends on…":** Lower prices are only **partially important** to Cineworld's recovery. **It depends on** (a) how price-sensitive cinema-goers are post-pandemic — recent data suggests they value the experience over price, so a small price cut may not boost demand much; and (b) whether Cineworld pairs the price change with other strategies, like improved snack offers or premium-format upselling. In the **short term**, lower prices may stem the customer loss; but **in the long term**, returning to profit will depend more on cutting costs (renegotiating leases, reducing locations) than on dropping ticket prices alone.

### What earns each mark band

| Level | Marks | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | 1-3 | Knowledge only |
| L2 | 4-7 | Two PEEL paragraphs with some application; conclusion is summary only |
| L3 | 8-12 | Two fully-developed PEELs using case data + "It depends on…" conclusion that brings **NEW evaluative content** (magnitude, timing, conditional reasoning) |

## 5. SPaG (Spelling, Punctuation, Grammar) — business-specific

There are no separate SPaG marks on this paper, but examiners' reports flag that markers reward **professional written communication** in extended responses. To stay on the right side of this:

- **Name the business in every paragraph.** Write "Adikoggz" not "the business"; "Cineworld plc" not "they"; "Greggs' bakeries" not "the shops". Examiners specifically warn that "the manager should…" loses easy AO2 marks.
- **Use business terminology fluently.** Use *gross profit margin* not "the profit %"; *organic growth* not "growing slowly"; *bar-gate stock graph* not "the spiky chart"; *break-even* not "the point where money is even".
- **Avoid contractions in extended responses.** Write "do not" / "cannot" / "will not", not "don't", "can't", "won't". This is a low-cost professionalism signal.
- **Use linking words / connectives** to make your chain of reasoning visible: *because, therefore, as a result, leads to, this means that, consequently, however, on the other hand, in the long term*.
- **Spell out abbreviations the first time.** "Average rate of return (ARR)…", then ARR thereafter.
- **One idea per sentence** in the 9-mark and 12-mark questions. Long, comma-spliced sentences are harder for the marker to parse and harder for you to develop an explicit chain of reasoning.

## 6. Common mistakes to avoid (from examiner reports 2019-2024)

> [!warning] These exact mistakes are flagged year after year. Don't make them.
> 1. **Treating Justify as a balanced essay.** Justify wants ONE side defended, not pros-and-cons of both. Use the rejected option as a one-line foil to **strengthen** the chosen one.
> 2. **Writing "the manager" / "the business" / "the owner" instead of using the named business.** This automatically caps Application (AO2) marks. Italicised names in the question = an explicit AO2 trigger.
> 3. **Skipping the "It depends on…" conclusion** on the 12-marker. A summary-only conclusion caps you at Level 2.
> 4. **Mis-reading bar-gate stock graphs.** Common errors: forgetting to subtract buffer stock when reading reorder level; mis-counting days between deliveries; not distinguishing the maximum stock from the reorder level.
> 5. **Failing to show working on calculations.** Method marks are awarded even when the final figure is wrong. Always write the formula, then the substitution, then the answer (with units and to the requested decimal places).
> 6. **State / Outline questions answered generically.** "Explain one stakeholder of [business]" → "customers" alone scores 0; "customers, who buy Greggs' sausage rolls and provide its main revenue" scores both marks.
> 7. **Memorised template paragraphs.** Markers spot pre-rehearsed paragraphs that don't fit the case context. Always tailor to the specific business named in the question.
> 8. **Padding the 3-mark Explain with no chain of reasoning.** "Explain one impact" needs *two* development steps, not just a one-word answer with surrounding words. Use BLT linking words ("Because… Leads to… To…") to make the two strands visible.

## 7. The "Almost Certain" topics list

These appear on **80%+ of papers** in the 2019-2024 dataset. Drill these first — the marginal hour spent here yields more marks than learning anything new.

### Paper 1 (Theme 1)
1. **Marketing mix (1.4.3)** — appears every paper, often in 6-mark or 9-mark form
2. **Sources of finance (1.3.5)** — every year, MCQ + extended question
3. **Technology and business (1.5.2)** — every year as 3-mark or 6-mark
4. **Cash flow / break-even calculations (1.3.2-1.3.3)** — at least one calc each year
5. **Business ownership types (1.4.1)** — sole trader / partnership / Ltd contrast
6. **Stakeholders (1.5.1)** — every year, often 3-mark Explain
7. **Market research methods (1.2.2)** — primary vs secondary appears most years
8. **9-mark Justify** with two named options for the entrepreneur to choose between

### Paper 2 (Theme 2)
1. **Bar-gate stock graphs (2.3.2)** — 5/6 live papers, expect again in 2026
2. **Gross profit / net profit / GP margin / NP margin (2.4.1)** — every year, multiple marks
3. **Average rate of return (2.4.1)** — every other year (2019, 2022, 2024 → due 2026)
4. **Average calculations** — 4/4 of the most recent papers
5. **Marketing mix application (2.2.x)** — every year as Discuss or Justify
6. **Organisational structure (2.5.1)** — hierarchical vs flat features regularly
7. **Methods of growth (2.1.1)** — internal vs external growth, mergers/takeovers
8. **9-mark Justify Option 1 vs Option 2** at Q6c AND Q7d — TWO of these per paper
