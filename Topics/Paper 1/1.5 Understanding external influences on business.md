# 1.5 Understanding external influences on business

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 1.5 covers stakeholders (1.5.1), technology (1.5.2), legislation (1.5.3), the economy (1.5.4) and broader external influences (1.5.5). Almost every Section C 12-mark Evaluate frames an external influence question.

## What this topic covers
- **Stakeholders** — owners, employees, customers, suppliers, the local community, government, pressure groups — and how their interests can conflict
- **Technology** — e-commerce, social media, payment systems, digital communication and how they reshape the marketing mix and operations
- **Legislation** — consumer law (Sale of Goods, Consumer Rights Act) and employment law (contracts, discrimination, minimum wage)
- **The economy** — interest rates, exchange rates, inflation, unemployment, consumer income — and how each affects costs, demand and profit
- Broader influences (government, environment, ethics)

## Key terms
- **Stakeholder**: any individual or group with an interest in the business
- **Pressure group**: an organisation that campaigns for a cause (e.g. Greenpeace)
- **E-commerce**: buying and selling online
- **Consumer law**: protects buyers (goods must be of satisfactory quality, as described, fit for purpose)
- **Employment law**: protects workers (minimum wage, anti-discrimination, fair contracts)
- **Interest rate**: the price of borrowing money (set by Bank of England base rate)
- **Exchange rate**: the price of one currency in another (e.g. £1 = €1.15)
- **Inflation**: a sustained rise in the general price level
- **Recession**: two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth
- **Disposable income**: income left after tax — drives consumer spending

## Substantive content

**Stakeholders (1.5.1).** Owners want profit; employees want pay/security; customers want low price/high quality; suppliers want timely payment; community wants jobs without disruption; government wants tax + lawful behaviour. Conflicts are common — e.g. owners cutting wages boosts profit but demotivates staff (June 2022 Discuss: conflict between owners and employees).

**Technology (1.5.2) — appears every year.** *E-commerce* lowers entry barriers — Aphrodite (2019), Adikoggz (2024) reach customers without a shop. *Social media* (Lili Heating, 2023) promotes for very low cost but exposes the business to bad reviews (On Your Bike, Nov 2020). *Payment systems* (PayPal, contactless, digital wallets) speed up sales and unlock online customers (Loco, Nov 2020). *Digital communication* (Zoom, Slack) enables flexible working. **Cost**: tech investment, training, cyber-security risk.

**Legislation (1.5.3).** Consumer law: must offer refunds, accurate descriptions, safe products. Employment law: written contracts, equal pay, anti-discrimination, minimum wage (£11.44 from April 2024, rising regularly), max working hours. Compliance raises costs but builds trust; non-compliance risks fines and reputation damage. Nov 2021 Discuss: impact of new employment laws on small business.

**The economy (1.5.4).** *Interest rates rise* → loans/mortgages dearer → consumer spending falls → demand falls (especially for non-essentials). For the business itself, existing loan repayments rise. *Exchange rate*: strong pound = imports cheaper, exports dearer (hurts UK exporters like POD Point). *Inflation* squeezes margins as input costs rise faster than the business can raise prices. *Unemployment rising* → demand falls but recruitment is easier and wages may flatten. *Consumer income falling* → demand for luxuries collapses (June 2024 Q7e: consumer income evaluation).

**External influences (1.5.5).** Government (taxes, regulations, support schemes), environment (sustainability pressure, carbon costs), ethics (Fairtrade, animal welfare).

> [!tip] 12-mark Evaluate magnet
> Q7(e) is often framed as an external influence ("Evaluate the importance of consumer income to…", 2024). Always end with "It depends on…" + 1–2 contextual factors (size of business, time horizon, scale of the change).

## Common exam pitfalls
- Discussing a stakeholder *group* without naming who's in it for the case study
- Saying "technology" generically — say which technology (social media? e-commerce? digital payments?) and how it changes the business
- On exchange rates, mixing up direction (strong pound = exports DEARER for foreign buyers)
