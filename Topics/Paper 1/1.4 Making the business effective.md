# 1.4 Making the business effective

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 1.4 covers business ownership types (1.4.1), location (1.4.2), the marketing mix (1.4.3) and the business plan (1.4.4). The marketing mix is the **single highest-marks** topic on Paper 1 (~9 marks/paper avg).

## What this topic covers
- The types of business ownership (sole trader, partnership, private limited company, franchise) and their pros/cons (especially **liability**)
- Factors influencing **location** (proximity to market, labour, materials, competitors, costs)
- The **marketing mix** (4 Ps): Product, Price, Place, Promotion — how each P interacts and the impact of digital/e-commerce
- What goes into a **business plan** and why it matters

## Key terms
- **Sole trader**: one owner, unincorporated, **unlimited liability**, keeps all profit
- **Partnership**: 2+ owners share capital and profit; usually unlimited liability
- **Private limited company (Ltd)**: shareholders, **limited liability**, "Ltd" suffix, shares not on public market
- **Franchise**: franchisee buys the right to trade under a franchisor's brand using their model
- **Limited liability**: owner only loses what they invested — personal assets safe
- **Unlimited liability**: owner is personally liable for all business debts
- **Location**: where the business operates (premises, factory, warehouse)
- **Marketing mix**: combination of Product, Price, Place, Promotion
- **Product life cycle**: introduction → growth → maturity → decline
- **Promotion**: communicating with customers (advertising, social media, sales promotion)
- **Place**: distribution channel — shop, online, wholesale
- **Business plan**: written document setting out the idea, aims, market, finance, operations

## Substantive content

**Ownership types (1.4.1).** Sole trader: simple to set up, full control, but unlimited liability and limited capital. Partnership: shared workload/skills, but disputes possible. Ltd: limited liability protects personal assets and makes raising capital easier — but more admin, accounts must be public, profit shared with shareholders. Franchise: lower risk (proven brand, training), but franchise fees and less control (Little Movers, 2022).

**Business location (1.4.2).** Influencing factors: proximity to **market** (cafés need foot traffic), proximity to **labour** (skilled staff), **materials/suppliers**, **competitors** (cluster vs avoid), **costs** (rent), and **internet** (a website lets a business locate anywhere — e.g. Sports Tours Ltd, 2021).

**The marketing mix (1.4.3).** The 4 Ps must work together. **Product**: design, quality, branding, USP. **Price**: cost-plus, competitive pricing, price skimming, penetration. **Place**: physical store vs online vs wholesale — e-commerce dramatically cuts entry cost (Aphrodite 2019, e-commerce). **Promotion**: advertising, social media, sponsorship, sales promotion, PR. Technology has reshaped every P — digital products, dynamic pricing, online place, social-media promotion. The marketing mix should match the **target segment** identified in 1.2.3.

**The business plan (1.4.4).** Sections typically include: executive summary, idea, market research, USP, marketing mix, operations, financial forecasts (cash flow, break-even), funding required. Purpose: clarify thinking, persuade lenders/investors, set targets to monitor against. Limitation: forecasts can be over-optimistic and the plan can become outdated.

> [!example] Examiner application example
> Last Course Patisserie (June 2019) became a Ltd to **expand premises** and protect the founders' personal assets — a Ltd structure made it easier to attract investors and reduce risk.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Saying "limited liability" when the case study is a sole trader (sole traders have **un**limited liability)
- Treating the 4 Ps as a list — examiners want links *between* them ("low price means promotion through deals not premium ads")
- Generic location answers — name the *specific* business and *specific* customer base
