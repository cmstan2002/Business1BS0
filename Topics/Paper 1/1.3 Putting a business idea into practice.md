# 1.3 Putting a business idea into practice

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 1.3 covers business aims and objectives (1.3.1), revenue/costs/profit/loss (1.3.2), break-even (1.3.3), cash and cash flow (1.3.4) and sources of finance (1.3.5). This is the **calculation-heavy** section of Paper 1.

## What this topic covers
- Setting **financial** (survival, profit, sales, market share, growth) and **non-financial** (independence, satisfaction, challenge, social) aims
- Calculating revenue, fixed/variable/total costs, profit and loss
- Drawing/reading break-even charts and calculating BEP and margin of safety
- Cash-flow forecasts: inflows, outflows, net cash flow, opening/closing balance
- Sources of finance for a start-up: personal savings, family/friends, bank loan, overdraft, trade credit, share capital, retained profit, crowdfunding

## Key terms
- **Aim**: long-term goal of a business
- **Objective**: a specific, measurable target that supports an aim (SMART)
- **Revenue**: money earned from sales (price × quantity)
- **Fixed costs**: costs that do not change with output (rent, salaries)
- **Variable costs**: costs that rise with output (raw materials, packaging)
- **Total costs**: TFC + TVC
- **Profit/Loss**: revenue − total costs (positive = profit)
- **Break-even point**: level of output where total revenue = total costs
- **Margin of safety**: actual sales − break-even sales
- **Cash flow**: movement of money into and out of the business
- **Net cash flow**: inflows − outflows in a period
- **Closing balance**: opening balance + net cash flow
- **Insolvency**: inability to pay debts as they fall due (often = business failure)
- **Trade credit**: supplier lets you pay later (typically 30–90 days)

## Substantive content

**Aims and objectives (1.3.1).** Financial aims: survival (most important for new firms — Aphrodite, 2019), profit, sales, market share, financial security. Non-financial: personal satisfaction, independence, challenge, social objectives (e.g. Ripple's consumer-owned wind farm).

**Revenue, costs, profit (1.3.2).** Revenue = price × quantity. Total cost = fixed + variable. Profit = revenue − total cost. Loss is the opposite. Examiners often hide the calc inside a small data table — work line-by-line.

**Break-even (1.3.3).** Formula: BEP (units) = fixed cost ÷ (selling price − variable cost per unit). Margin of safety = actual sales − BEP. Lowering BEP can be done by cutting fixed costs, cutting variable cost per unit, or raising price (each has trade-offs — e.g. raising price might reduce demand). On Your Bike (Nov 2020) was asked to analyse reducing costs to lower BEP.

**Cash and cash flow (1.3.4).** Cash ≠ profit — a profitable business can still run out of cash if customers pay late. A cash-flow forecast lists monthly inflows (sales, loans), outflows (rent, wages, stock) and computes net cash flow + closing balance. Negative closing balance = need for an overdraft or extra finance. *June 2019 Discuss*: "the importance of cash to the survival of a small business" — answer: bills must be paid even if profitable, no cash = insolvency.

**Sources of finance (1.3.5).** Internal: personal savings (cheap, limited), retained profit (no interest, only if profitable). External: family/friends (informal, can damage relationships), bank loan (predictable repayment, interest cost), overdraft (flexible, expensive), trade credit (free short-term, supplier may refuse repeat), share capital (only Ltd; sells ownership), crowdfunding (good for niche/values brands; success not guaranteed). Adikoggz (2024) faced an overdraft vs trade credit Justify — overdraft = flexible but high interest; trade credit = free but ties you to one supplier.

> [!warning] Calculation rule
> Show every step. Examiners give **method marks** even when the final figure is wrong. A blank workings box caps the mark.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Mixing up cash and profit ("profit will pay the wages this month" — wrong, cash will)
- Forgetting units in BEP answers (number of units, not £)
- Listing sources without saying which is most appropriate for the *case-study* business
