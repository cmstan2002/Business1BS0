# 1.2 Spotting a business opportunity

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 1.2 covers customer needs (1.2.1), market research methods (1.2.2), market segmentation (1.2.3), and the competitive environment (1.2.4) — how a small business identifies the gap it can fill.

## What this topic covers
- Why understanding **customer needs** (price, quality, choice, convenience) is the foundation of every successful business
- **Market research** — primary (own data) vs secondary (existing data); quantitative vs qualitative; using social media and big data
- **Market segmentation** — splitting the market by demographics (age, gender, income), location, lifestyle, income
- **The competitive environment** — strengths and weaknesses of competitors, market mapping

## Key terms
- **Customer needs**: what customers want from a product (price, quality, choice, convenience)
- **Market research**: collecting data about customers, competitors and market trends to inform decisions
- **Primary research**: first-hand data the business collects itself (surveys, interviews, observation, focus groups)
- **Secondary research**: existing data from other sources (government stats, market reports, competitor websites)
- **Quantitative data**: numerical data (e.g. 70% of customers prefer X)
- **Qualitative data**: opinion-based data (e.g. customer feedback in their own words)
- **Market segmentation**: dividing customers into groups with shared characteristics
- **Market map**: a 2-axis diagram (e.g. price vs quality) showing where competitors sit and where gaps exist
- **USP**: a feature making a business different from rivals
- **Competitive environment**: the conditions a business faces from rival firms

## Substantive content

**Customer needs (1.2.1).** Four classic needs: **price** (affordable), **quality** (fit for purpose), **choice** (variety), **convenience** (easy to access). Get these wrong and customers walk to a rival. Adikoggz (2024) succeeded by meeting demand for *customised* trainers — a need mainstream brands underserved.

**Market research (1.2.2).** *Primary*: surveys (cheap, can be biased), focus groups (rich qualitative insight, expensive), observation, trial products. *Secondary*: government population data, ONS, competitor pricing pages, trade journals — fast and cheap but not specific. Modern methods include **social media analytics** (free, real-time) and **big data** (large data sets to spot patterns). Lili Heating (2023) used the *internet for secondary research* to identify gaps for a female-led plumbing service.

**Market segmentation (1.2.3).** Common bases: age (Gen Z vs over-65s), gender (Lili Heating targeted female customers), income (luxury vs budget), location (Sports Tours targeted UK-based youth teams), lifestyle (vegan, eco-conscious). Segmentation lets a small business focus marketing spend instead of wasting it on the general public.

**Competitive environment (1.2.4).** Look at competitors' strengths (brand, scale, price) and weaknesses (poor service, narrow range). A **market map** (e.g. axes: price vs quality) shows where the market is crowded and where the gaps are. PFC (2024) faced large gym chains; their gap was personal, individual service. Strong competition = pressure on price, the need for a USP, faster innovation, and risk of closing.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Saying "do market research" without specifying *primary or secondary* and *which method*
- Confusing segmentation with targeting — segmentation = grouping; targeting = choosing one group
- On the competitive environment, describing the *industry* generally instead of the *specific rivals* in the case study
