# 2.5 Making human resource decisions

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 2.5 covers organisational structure (2.5.1), recruitment and selection (2.5.2), training and development (2.5.3), and motivation (2.5.4) — financial vs non-financial. HR is the **single largest topic on Paper 2** (often 14–22 marks/paper).

## What this topic covers
- **Organisational structures**: hierarchical vs flat, centralised vs decentralised, chain of command, span of control
- **Effective communication** and the impact of barriers (too many layers, unclear messages)
- **Different ways of working**: full-time vs part-time, permanent vs temporary, flexible hours, remote/home working
- **Recruitment**: internal vs external; recruitment **documents** (job description, person specification, application form, CV, cover letter)
- **Training**: induction, on-the-job, off-the-job; ongoing development
- **Motivation**: financial (pay, bonus, commission, fringe benefits) and non-financial (job rotation/enrichment, autonomy, praise)

## Key terms
- **Hierarchical structure**: many layers, narrow span of control, clear chain of command
- **Flat structure**: few layers, wide span of control, faster decisions
- **Centralised**: decisions made at the top
- **Decentralised**: decisions delegated to local managers
- **Chain of command**: line of authority from top to bottom
- **Span of control**: number of subordinates a manager directly oversees
- **Job description**: tasks and responsibilities of a role
- **Person specification**: skills/qualifications/experience the candidate needs
- **Induction training**: introduction for new starters
- **On-the-job training**: learning while doing, in the workplace
- **Off-the-job training**: away from work (college, course, conference)
- **Remuneration**: total reward — pay + benefits
- **Job enrichment**: adding meaningful, challenging tasks to a role
- **Retention**: keeping staff and reducing turnover

## Substantive content

**Organisational structure (2.5.1).** Hierarchical: clear progression, but slow decisions, expensive layers (traditional supermarkets). Flat: faster, cheaper, empowers staff, but managers stretched (start-ups, Google). Centralised: consistency across branches; decentralised: responsive to local market (Iceland gives store managers some discretion). Communication needs to flow down and up — long chains distort messages.

**Ways of working.** Full-time = stability and loyalty but higher cost. Part-time/flexible = lower cost, attracts parents/students but less consistency. Permanent = security and motivation; temporary = flexibility for the firm but high turnover. **Remote working** (Facebook 2023 Q7e) cuts office costs and widens talent pool but weakens collaboration and culture. Popeyes (2024) Q6c framed flexible-hours vs full-time as the trade-off.

**Recruitment and selection (2.5.2).** *Internal*: cheap, motivating, you know the candidate — but no fresh ideas. *External*: wider pool, fresh thinking, but expensive and unknown fit. Recruitment documents: **job description** (the role) → **person specification** (the person) → **application form / CV** → **interview / test** → **offer**. Recruitment-document MCQs appear regularly (2019, 2023, 2024).

**Training (2.5.3).** *Induction* shortens time to competence. *On-the-job* is cheap and immediately relevant; *off-the-job* gives broader skills but is costlier and removes worker from role. Investing in training raises productivity and retention but raises short-term cost (Sainsbury's 2019 Q7d: retraining vs increase wages).

**Motivation (2.5.4).** **Financial**: hourly pay, salary, commission, bonus, profit sharing, fringe benefits (pension, gym). **Non-financial**: job rotation (variety), job enrichment (meaningful tasks), autonomy, praise, fair treatment, good working environment. Highly motivated staff = higher productivity, fewer absences, better customer service, higher retention.

> [!example] Justify framing
> Recent HR Justifies: retraining vs wages (2019), permanent vs temporary contracts (2020), flexible vs full-time hours (2024). Pattern: pick the option that fits the firm's *cost pressure* and *demand pattern*; reject the other in one line.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Saying "motivate them with money" — examiners want a *named* method (commission, profit-share) and the trade-off (cost rises)
- Confusing **job description** (tasks) and **person specification** (person traits)
- Treating "flat structure = always better" — depends on firm size and complexity
