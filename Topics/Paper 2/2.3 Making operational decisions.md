# 2.3 Making operational decisions

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 2.3 covers business operations (2.3.1 — production processes, technology in operations), working with suppliers (2.3.2 — bar-gate stock, JIT, procurement), managing quality (2.3.3) and the sales process / customer service (2.3.4). **Bar-gate stock graphs appear in 5 of 6 recent live papers.**

## What this topic covers
- **Production processes**: job, batch, flow — and when each suits the business
- The **role of procurement** — choosing suppliers, building reliable supplier relationships
- **Stock management** using a **bar-gate (re-order) graph**: buffer stock, re-order level, lead time, max stock
- **Just-in-Time (JIT)** stock control vs holding buffer stock
- **Quality control** (inspect at the end) vs **quality assurance** (build in at every stage); TQM
- The **sales process** stages and the importance of high-quality **customer service** and after-sales

## Key terms
- **Job production**: one-off, bespoke (Fender custom guitar, SAMs)
- **Batch production**: groups of identical items (Greggs sausage rolls)
- **Flow production**: continuous mass production (cars, soft drinks)
- **Procurement**: process of finding and buying inputs from suppliers
- **Buffer stock**: minimum stock kept to avoid running out
- **Re-order level**: stock level that triggers a new order
- **Lead time**: time between ordering and delivery
- **Just-in-Time (JIT)**: stock arrives only when needed, holding ~zero inventory
- **Quality control (QC)**: inspect finished output, reject faulty items
- **Quality assurance (QA)**: build quality checks into every stage of production
- **Customer service**: how a business looks after customers before, during and after sale
- **After-sales service**: support after purchase (returns, repairs, warranties)

## Substantive content

**Production processes (2.3.1).** *Job*: high quality, bespoke, expensive per unit, slow (Fender custom, plumbers, dressmakers). *Batch*: efficient for groups, allows variety (Greggs trays of pastries). *Flow*: lowest unit cost, capital-intensive, inflexible (Tesla assembly line, Coca-Cola). The right choice = matches demand pattern + variety needed.

**Suppliers and stock (2.3.2).** Choosing suppliers: cost, quality, reliability, flexibility, ethics. Poor supplier reliability cripples operations — KFC (Nov 2020) closed 900 stores when DHL failed. **Bar-gate stock graph** (read every Paper 2): horizontal axis = days; vertical axis = stock. Stock falls along a sloped line (usage), then jumps vertically when a delivery arrives. The horizontal **buffer-stock line** is the safety floor; the **re-order level** is the trigger; the **lead time** is the gap between ordering and delivery. *Order size = max stock − buffer stock.* **JIT** removes buffer stock — saves storage cost and waste, but breaks if a supplier is late (KFC).

**Managing quality (2.3.3).** **QC**: cheap to set up but rejects waste; faults reach customers. **QA**: every worker is responsible for quality; fewer defects, but training cost is higher. **TQM** (Total Quality Management): quality is a culture across the whole business. Strong quality protects brand and reduces returns/refunds — vital for premium brands (Tesla, Ocado).

**Sales process and customer service (2.3.4).** Stages: customer interest → product knowledge → speed and efficiency → post-sales service. Excellent customer service generates repeat custom, positive reviews and referrals; poor service spreads on social media (On Your Bike example). After-sales (warranties, repairs, refunds) builds long-term loyalty.

> [!warning] Bar-gate pitfall
> Examiners specifically flag that students forget to **subtract buffer stock from the re-order level** when asked for "size of order delivered" (Nov 2020 KFC chicken portions). Always read max stock − buffer stock for delivery size.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Confusing **batch** with **flow** — batch makes groups; flow is continuous
- JIT pros without naming the **trade-off** (no buffer = vulnerability)
- On the bar-gate graph: forgetting to subtract buffer when calculating delivery size
