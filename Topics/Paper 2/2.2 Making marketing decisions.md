# 2.2 Making marketing decisions

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 2.2 covers product (2.2.1 — design mix, product life cycle, extension strategies), price (2.2.2 — pricing strategies), promotion (2.2.3) and place (2.2.4 — distribution incl. e-commerce). At least 14 marks every paper.

## What this topic covers
- The **design mix**: function, aesthetics, cost — the trade-offs designers balance
- The **product life cycle** (introduction, growth, maturity, decline) and **extension strategies** (relaunch, new market, advertising, packaging change)
- **Pricing strategies**: cost-plus, competitive, penetration, skimming, price discrimination, psychological
- **Promotion**: advertising, sponsorship, social media, viral, sales promotion, PR
- **Place / distribution**: physical retailers, wholesalers, direct, e-commerce
- Using the **marketing mix** as a coherent strategy where all four Ps reinforce each other

## Key terms
- **Design mix**: balance of function (does it work?), aesthetics (does it look good?), cost (can it be made profitably?)
- **Product life cycle**: introduction → growth → maturity → decline
- **Extension strategy**: tactics to prolong maturity (relaunch, new packaging, new market)
- **Cost-plus pricing**: total cost + a fixed mark-up
- **Competitive pricing**: priced near rivals' levels
- **Penetration pricing**: low launch price to gain market share
- **Price skimming**: high launch price, falls over time (premium tech)
- **Sponsorship**: paying to associate the brand with an event/team
- **Viral advertising**: ad spreads peer-to-peer (KFC FCK apology, 2018)
- **E-commerce**: selling online
- **Distribution channel**: route a product takes from producer to consumer

## Substantive content

**Product (2.2.1).** The design mix forces a trade-off — high aesthetics + high function = high cost (Tesla Model 3). Greggs' vegan sausage roll (2022) was designed for *function* (taste, plant-based) at *low cost* and won mass appeal. The product life cycle: at *introduction* spending is high, sales low; *growth* sees fast sales rises; *maturity* is the stable peak; *decline* is when substitutes arrive. Extension strategies extend maturity — Apple iPhone's annual update cycle is a classic example.

**Price (2.2.2).** *Cost-plus*: simple, but ignores demand. *Competitive*: safe but reactive. *Penetration*: builds share fast (KFC entering UK, Popeyes 2024) — but eroded margin. *Skimming*: maximises early profit on innovations (PS5 launch). *Price discrimination*: charging different prices to different segments (peak/off-peak cinema). Cineworld (2024) Q7e: lower prices to return to profit — but lower price also lowers revenue per ticket if demand doesn't rise enough.

**Promotion (2.2.3).** *Advertising* (TV, print, digital) builds awareness; *social media* is cheap, targeted, two-way; *sponsorship* (JD Sports + sports stars, 2022) builds brand association; *viral* delivers reach for almost no spend but is unpredictable; *sales promotion* (BOGOF, vouchers) shifts stock fast. Choice depends on target segment and budget.

**Place (2.2.4).** Channels: producer → retailer → consumer (most common); producer → wholesaler → retailer (efficient for small retailers); producer → consumer (e-commerce, direct from factory). E-commerce has reshaped place — Ocado (2023) is a pure online grocer; ASOS (2021) ships online globally. Faster delivery is a competitive weapon (Ocado Q6c 2023).

> [!tip] Marketing-mix coherence
> In a 9-mark Justify or 12-mark Evaluate, never analyse one P in isolation. *Lower price* changes the *promotion* needed (no premium ads), the *place* (mass-market not boutique) and the *product* perception (mass not exclusive).

## Common exam pitfalls
- Treating PLC as the only product topic — design mix has been an MCQ in 5/7 papers
- Saying "lower prices = more profit" — only true if demand is **price elastic** and the volume rise outweighs the margin cut
- Confusing penetration (low launch price) with skimming (high launch price)
