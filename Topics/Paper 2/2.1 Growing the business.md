# 2.1 Growing the business

> [!info] Spec reference
> Topic 2.1 covers methods of business growth (2.1.1), changes in business aims as it grows (2.1.2), business and globalisation (2.1.3), and ethics + the environment as influences on growth (2.1.4). This topic powers most Section C plc Evaluates.

## What this topic covers
- **Internal (organic) growth** — new products, new markets, e-commerce — vs **external (inorganic) growth** — mergers, takeovers
- Why and how a private business becomes a **public limited company (plc)** through flotation
- **Sources of finance for growth**: retained profit, share capital, loan capital, selling assets
- How **aims and objectives change** as a business grows (survival → profit → growth → market leadership)
- **Globalisation** — selling/sourcing abroad, multinationals, tariffs and trade blocs
- **Ethics and the environment** — Fairtrade, sustainability, plastic, recycling, animal welfare

## Key terms
- **Organic growth**: growing using the firm's own resources (new products, new outlets)
- **Inorganic growth**: growing by combining with another firm (merger, takeover)
- **Merger**: two firms agree to combine into one
- **Takeover (acquisition)**: one firm buys a controlling stake in another
- **Public limited company (plc)**: shares traded publicly on a stock exchange
- **Flotation/IPO**: the first sale of shares to the public
- **Retained profit**: profit kept inside the business after tax/dividends
- **Share capital**: money raised by issuing shares
- **Loan capital**: long-term borrowing repaid with interest
- **Globalisation**: increasing integration of world economies
- **Tariff**: a tax on imported goods
- **Trade bloc**: group of countries with reduced trade barriers (e.g. EU)
- **Multinational**: a business operating in two or more countries

## Substantive content

**Methods of growth (2.1.1).** *Internal*: organic, lower-risk, slower (Popeyes' UK store-by-store rollout, 2024). *External*: faster, instant scale, but expensive and integration is hard (JD Sports' takeover of Footasylum, 2022; Sainsbury's takeover of Argos, SAMs). External growth gives instant market share and removes a competitor; risks include culture clashes and overpaying.

**Sources of finance for growth.** *Retained profit*: free, no dilution, but limited; *share capital*: raises huge sums but dilutes control (Tesla 2021 Justify); *loan capital*: keeps ownership but adds interest cost; *selling assets*: quick cash but reduces capacity (Cineworld 2024 Q7d).

**Going public (plc).** Pros: huge new capital, prestige, easier to grow further. Cons: takeover threat, public scrutiny, must publish accounts, short-term shareholder pressure. Greggs (2022), JD Sports, ASOS, Cineworld are all featured plcs.

**Changing aims and objectives (2.1.2).** Survival drops away; profit/sales/market share rise; ethical and environmental aims emerge as the brand needs to defend its reputation (Iceland's plastic ban, 2020; Sainsbury's "Fairly Traded", 2019).

**Globalisation (2.1.3).** Benefits: bigger market, cheaper production overseas, economies of scale (Tesla in Shanghai). Risks: tariffs raise import/export costs; exchange-rate volatility; competition from foreign rivals at home. **Trade blocs** (EU) cut tariffs *between* members; tariffs *on imports from outside* the bloc can hurt UK exporters since Brexit.

**Ethics and environment (2.1.4).** Ethical actions (Fairtrade, fair wages, reduced packaging) raise costs but build brand loyalty and protect against pressure-group action (Iceland, plastic ban). Environmental responsibility (recycling, lower carbon) increasingly expected — failing to act risks consumer backlash.

## Common exam pitfalls
- Confusing merger (agreed) with takeover (one buys the other)
- Calling a private Ltd a plc — Ltd shares are NOT publicly traded
- Listing globalisation pros/cons without saying which the *named* business benefits from
