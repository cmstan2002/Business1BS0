# Case Studies — Index

Every case study used in the Edexcel GCSE Business 1BS0 papers from 2019 to 2024, plus the specimen sets. Use this as a quick-reference cheat sheet when revising — knowing the *type* of business behind each Section B/C extract helps you pre-load the relevant business concepts.

> [!tip] Revision strategy
> For each case below, write a one-paragraph **profile** in your own words covering: (a) what the business does, (b) the dilemma in the exam, (c) the business concept being tested. Doing this for all 16 Paper 1 cases + all 12 Paper 2 cases takes ~3 hours and is the single most efficient AO2 booster in the run-up to the exam.

## Paper 1 — case study patterns

### Section B businesses (small UK firms with a physical location)

Section B is **always** a small UK business — sole trader, partnership, or small Ltd — usually with a physical premises and a relatable owner story. The exam will test ownership type, the marketing mix, sources of finance, business location, and basic finance arithmetic.

| Year | Business | Industry / type | Section B 9-mark Justify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specimen 1 | Meringue (Neil & Sue) | Bakery & cafe — partnership | Increase prices 10% vs cheaper supplier |
| Specimen 2 | Zoella Beauty | Online beauty brand (sole trader → expansion) | Increase promotion vs reduce prices |
| June 2019 | Last Course Patisserie | Food manufacturer (Ltd), looking to expand premises | Invest in new machinery vs employ more staff |
| Nov 2020 | On Your Bike | Family-owned bike shop + workshop + rental | Open a second shop vs use e-commerce |
| Nov 2021 | Sports Tours Ltd | Tour operator (online + international) — Ltd | Use professional venues vs top-class coaching |
| June 2022 | Little Movers | Children's classes / franchise model — Melanie (working mother) | Internet research on Polish competitors vs focus groups |
| June 2023 | Lili Heating Ltd | Female-founded plumbing / heating start-up | Discounted price for female customers vs use social media |
| June 2024 | Adikoggz | Customised trainers — sole trader, started as a hobby | Overdraft from bank vs trade credit from suppliers |

**Common Section B question types:** outline an advantage of [ownership type], analyse impact of [marketing channel] on [business], analyse impact of [external factor] on [business], 9-mark Justify between two operational/marketing tactics.

### Section C businesses (niche / tech / values-led start-ups)

Section C is the bigger 25-mark synoptic question, and the case is usually a **niche start-up** with a clear values angle (sustainability, tech, social) or a slightly larger young Ltd. The 9-mark Justify is often a financing or expansion choice; the 12-mark Evaluate spans 2 topics.

| Year | Business | Industry / type | Section C 9-mark + 12-mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specimen 1 | Sally | Sole-trader personal trainer | Buy a franchise / not buy a franchise (single-decision Justify) |
| Specimen 2 | POD Point | Tech / green energy (electric car charging) — Ltd, exports | Sell to foreign customers vs cut costs |
| June 2019 | Aphrodite | Clothing retail with e-commerce | Bank loan vs crowdfunding |
| Nov 2020 | Loco | Sole trader paddleboard manufacturer (niche sports) | Digital payments (PayPal) vs online meetings |
| Nov 2021 | That Feeling | Sole-trader barber shop with branded merchandise | Increase own-brand range vs open second shop |
| June 2022 | Digital Allies | B2B digital marketing services (Ltd, expanding) | Improve uni website vs design social media campaign |
| June 2023 | Ripple | Co-operative-style green energy / consumer-owned wind farm | Reduce small-business tax vs free training (govt policy choice) |
| June 2024 | PFC | Independent gym facing chain competition | Reduce membership price vs personal fitness plans |

**Common Section C question types:** State / Outline applied to the business, identify a value from a chart (Q7b), 2-mark Outline, 9-mark Justify between two strategic options, 12-mark Evaluate of importance/impact of a strategic decision.

### Recurring Paper 1 themes
- **Online / e-commerce / social media** featured in 11 of 16 cases
- **Expansion decision** (new shop, new market, franchise) features in 12 of 16
- **Founder personal story / non-financial aim** explicit in 10 of 16
- **Female / minority entrepreneur** focus: 3 of 8 Section B founders are female (Lili, Zoella, Melanie at Little Movers)

## Paper 2 — case study businesses

Paper 2 cases are **real, household-name businesses**. Section B is mid-cap (you've heard of it; it has 100s-1000s of staff). Section C is plc / multinational.

### Paper 2 Section B (mid-cap, real businesses)

#### Mind Candy (June 2019)
- **Industry/sector:** UK games and apps developer (creator of Moshi Monsters, then Petlandia personalised children's books).
- **Year(s):** June 2019.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — fund expansion via **retained profit** vs **share capital**.
- **Concepts tested:** sources of finance for growth, retained profit vs equity dilution, growth strategy for a creative-tech business, importance of brand and intellectual property.

#### KFC (Nov 2020)
- **Industry/sector:** US/UK fast-food chain (~900 UK restaurants), supply-chain dependent on DHL.
- **Year(s):** Nov 2020.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — boost sales via **lower prices** vs **viral advertising**. Also calculations from a bar-gate chicken-portions stock graph including **days out of stock** after the 2018 DHL supply failure.
- **Concepts tested:** stock control, supplier relationships, JIT vs buffer stock, viral marketing, price elasticity, brand reputation crisis management.

#### Tesla (Nov 2021)
- **Industry/sector:** US/global electric vehicle manufacturer (Model 3, Shanghai factory, Elon Musk).
- **Year(s):** Nov 2021.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — fund expansion via **share capital** vs **loan capital**. Calculations on Tesla price reductions 2017-2019 (% change, average price).
- **Concepts tested:** sources of finance for growth, globalisation, tariffs, multinationals, organic growth, pressure groups (environmental).

#### Greggs (June 2022)
- **Industry/sector:** UK food-to-go chain (~1,900 shops, vegan sausage roll launch), plc.
- **Year(s):** June 2022.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — **differentiate the product range** vs **lower prices**. Discuss on retained profit. ARR calculation.
- **Concepts tested:** retained profit as a source of finance, ARR, differentiation, design mix, environmental impact, motivated employees.

#### Ocado (June 2023)
- **Industry/sector:** UK online grocery + warehouse-automation tech, plc.
- **Year(s):** June 2023.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — **faster delivery service** vs **expand product range**. Calculate sales revenue from GP + cost of sales.
- **Concepts tested:** flow production / automation, customer service / delivery speed, supplier relationships, NP margin, e-commerce, JIT.

#### Popeyes (June 2024)
- **Industry/sector:** US fast-food (Louisiana-style fried chicken), expanding into the UK.
- **Year(s):** June 2024.
- **Exam dilemma:** 9-mark Justify — employ workers on **flexible hours** vs **full-time**. ARR calculation with explicit time horizon. Bar-gate stock graph (days to run out).
- **Concepts tested:** internal growth (UK expansion), HR contracts / flexible vs full-time, ARR, JIT, customer service, recruitment documents, motivation methods.

### Paper 2 Section C (plc / multinational)

#### Sainsbury's (Specimen 1 & June 2019)
- **Industry/sector:** UK supermarket plc; took over Argos / Home Retail Group; switched from Fairtrade to its own "Fairly Traded" scheme.
- **Year(s):** Specimen Set 1 (takeover), June 2019 (ethics).
- **Exam dilemma:** Specimen — Evaluate whether Sainsbury's will benefit from the Argos takeover (external growth). 2019 — Evaluate impact of replacing Fairtrade with a private "Fairly Traded" scheme (ethics + brand reputation). Section C 9-mark: **retraining** vs **increase wages**.
- **Concepts tested:** external growth (mergers and takeovers), ethics and CSR, brand reputation, stakeholder conflicts (suppliers / pressure groups), motivation (training vs pay).

#### Iceland (Nov 2020)
- **Industry/sector:** UK frozen-food supermarket; first major UK retailer to pledge to ban plastic packaging on own-brand.
- **Year(s):** Nov 2020.
- **Exam dilemma:** 12-mark Evaluate — whether Iceland will benefit from its plastic-packaging ban. 9-mark Justify — **permanent contracts** vs **temporary contracts**.
- **Concepts tested:** environmental impact, pressure groups, ethics, costs vs reputation, HR contracts, brand differentiation.

#### ASOS (Nov 2021)
- **Industry/sector:** UK online fashion retailer plc; ~£3bn revenue; warehouse logistics + heavy social-media marketing.
- **Year(s):** Nov 2021.
- **Exam dilemma:** 12-mark Evaluate — whether high-quality logistics will give ASOS competitive advantage. 9-mark Justify — **lower prices** vs **advertise on more social-media channels**. Line-graph share-price reading.
- **Concepts tested:** competitive advantage via operations, e-commerce, social media marketing, share price as performance indicator, pricing strategy, design mix.

#### JD Sports (June 2022)
- **Industry/sector:** UK athleisure retailer plc / multinational; took over Footasylum.
- **Year(s):** June 2022.
- **Exam dilemma:** 12-mark Evaluate — whether the Footasylum takeover will allow JD Sports to **increase profit**. 9-mark Justify — **targeted online advertising** vs **sponsorship**. Pie chart of social-media customer source.
- **Concepts tested:** external growth (takeovers), promotion methods, sponsorship, competition law (Competition and Markets Authority briefly relevant), customer segmentation by social-media platform.

#### Facebook (June 2023)
- **Industry/sector:** US tech multinational (now Meta); Oculus VR; remote-working employer.
- **Year(s):** June 2023.
- **Exam dilemma:** 12-mark Evaluate — impact on Facebook of allowing remote working. 9-mark Justify — **grow internally** vs **grow externally**. Identify which company left the VR market.
- **Concepts tested:** organic vs inorganic growth, remote working / HR flexibility, motivation, communication, multinational operations, competition in emerging markets (VR).

#### Cineworld (June 2024)
- **Industry/sector:** UK / global cinema chain plc; carrying ~$8.9bn debt; post-pandemic loss-recovery.
- **Year(s):** June 2024.
- **Exam dilemma:** 12-mark Evaluate — importance of charging **lower prices** to allow Cineworld to return to profit. 9-mark Justify — **selling assets** vs **issuing new share capital**. Horizontal market-share bar chart.
- **Concepts tested:** pricing strategy, sources of finance for survival, market share, debt management, post-pandemic recovery, loss vs profit, environmental impact.

## Cross-reference summary

| Paper | Section | Most common business type | Most common dilemma |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Section B | Sole trader / Ltd with physical premises (cafe, gym, mobile trades, retail) | Internal tactical / operational choice — pricing, marketing channel, finance source |
| Paper 1 | Section C | Niche / values-led / tech start-up | Expansion route or finance source choice |
| Paper 2 | Section B | Real mid-cap UK/US brand (food chain, manufacturer, retailer) | Operational choice — pricing, advertising, contracts, growth method |
| Paper 2 | Section C | UK plc / multinational | Strategic decision — takeover, ethics, growth route, recovery |

> [!example] Predicted 2026 cases (from Paper 1 analysis)
> - **Paper 1 Section B** likely a service business with physical location: cafe, restaurant, mobile dog grooming, tutoring, or repair/trades. Hospitality is overdue (last 2019).
> - **Paper 1 Section C** likely a sustainable / ethical / tech-forward small business — possibly **AI-enabled** or **influencer / online creator** echoing the Zoella specimen.
> - **Paper 2 Section B** likely another household-name retailer or food-service brand.
> - **Paper 2 Section C** likely a plc dilemma — **sustainability**, **takeover**, or **strategic pivot post-disruption**. A pie chart in Section C is overdue.
